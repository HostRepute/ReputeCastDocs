Gmail 550 5.7.1 is a permanent refusal that Gmail uses for several reasons: the message looks unsolicited, the sending
IP address or domain has a very low reputation, or a policy forbids the message. Gmail's text tells you which one it is,
and each needs a different fix.

## What Gmail means

These are the texts newsletter senders meet most often with 550 5.7.1:

> This message is likely unsolicited email. To reduce the amount of spam sent to Gmail, this message has been blocked.

> This message is likely suspicious due to the very low reputation of the sending IP address. To best protect our users
> from spam, the message has been blocked.

The same text exists for "the very low reputation of the sending domain". Other 550 5.7.1 texts include:

- "The user or domain that you are sending to (or from) has a policy that prohibits the email that you sent." Gmail adds
  `gcdp` to errors that come from a custom rule a Google Workspace administrator created.
- "The IP you're using to send email is not authorized to send email directly to our servers. Use the SMTP relay at your
  service provider instead."
- "This message is not RFC 5322 compliant", with the header that is missing, duplicated or malformed.
- "This email has been rate limited."

Source: [Gmail SMTP errors and codes](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/support/troubleshooting/gmail-smtp-errors-and-codes),
Google Workspace Help. Checked October 2026.

## Why it happens

- **Likely unsolicited.** Gmail's filters judge the mail as unwanted: contacts who didn't opt in or stopped reading, a
  high spam rate, or content that looks like spam.
- **Low reputation.** The sending IP address or domain has a history of unwanted mail, or is new and sends a lot at once.
- **Policy.** The recipient's Google Workspace administrator blocks the message (`gcdp`), or Gmail doesn't accept direct
  delivery from the IP address, which can happen when you send from your own server.
- **Headers.** The message breaks RFC 5322, for example with a missing or duplicate From header.

## How ReputeCast classifies it

The [bounce class](/reference/glossary/#bounce-class) follows Gmail's text:

| Gmail's text                                                                             | Bounce class   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Likely unsolicited email, or not RFC 5322 compliant (both mention spam)                   | `spam_block`   |
| Very low reputation of the sending IP address or domain                                  | `reputation`   |
| A policy prohibits the email, the IP isn't authorized, rate limited, or the bare code    | `policy_block` |

All three classes share the report row <UiPath path="reports.bounceType.blocked" />. The refusal is about the sender, so
most built-in adapters record a soft bounce and nothing is suppressed; some sending providers report it as permanent and
then the address is suppressed. See
[when ReputeCast suppresses an address](/smtp-errors/#when-reputecast-suppresses-an-address).

## Find the cause and fix it

<Steps>

1. Read Gmail's text. Open <UiPath path="nav.providers > providers.tabEvents" />, filter
   <UiPath path="providers.events.typeFilter" /> to <UiPath path="providers.events.optionSoftBounce" /> or
   <UiPath path="providers.events.optionHardBounce" />, and search for a Gmail recipient.
   <UiPath path="providers.events.colCode" /> shows which 550 5.7.1 you got.
2. For **likely unsolicited**, look at who received the campaign. Send only to contacts with consent, exclude those who
   stopped opening ([keep your list healthy](/audience/list-health/)), and check the
   [content warnings](/campaigns/content-checks/) the composer shows.
3. For **low reputation**, open <UiPath path="nav.deliverability > placement.health" />: check the domain and IP health
   and whether the IP address or domain is [on a blocklist](/deliverability/blocklists/). If the IP address or domain is
   new, slow down and [warm it up](/providers/warm-up/).
4. For a **policy** with `gcdp`, the recipient's organization blocks the message. Nothing in your setup causes it; the
   recipient can ask their administrator to allow your mail.
5. For **IP not authorized**, send through a sending provider instead of delivering straight from your own server, or
   relay your server's mail through one.
6. For **not RFC 5322 compliant**, look at the header Gmail names. If you send through the
   [generic HTTP adapter](/adapters/generic-http/) or a community adapter, check the headers its mapping adds.

</Steps>

## Check that it worked

- The next campaign's report has fewer or no 550 5.7.1 entries in <UiPath path="reports.colCodes" />.
- Deliverability shows no blocklist listing for your sending IP addresses and domains.

## Troubleshooting

- **Only one sending provider's mail is blocked.** Its IP addresses are the likely cause. Route the Gmail share through
  another sending provider with a [route](/providers/routing/) while you fix it.
- **You see both the unsolicited and the low-reputation texts.** Work through step 2 before step 3: both texts say Gmail
  is protecting its users from spam.
- **Gmail's complaints are high too.** Read [bounces, complaints and provider limits](/deliverability/bounces-and-complaints/).
