Yahoo TSS04 is a temporary deferral: Yahoo's servers answer `421 4.7.0` with the tag `[TSS04]` when they want less mail
from you for now, most often because of complaints from Yahoo users or a sudden rise in volume. Your sending provider
retries later. This page explains what Yahoo looks at and how to bring the deferrals down.

## What Yahoo means

Yahoo's servers reply with a line such as:

```text title="SMTP reply"
421 4.7.0 [TSS04] Messages from 203.0.113.25 temporarily deferred due to unexpected volume or user complaints - 4.16.55.1; see https://postmaster.yahooinc.com/error-codes
```

Yahoo's Sender Hub describes these "TS" errors as message temporarily deferred:

> This is a temporary error and your mail server may automatically re-try sending the email at a later time. Your message
> may have been deferred due to one or more of the following:
>
> - Emails from your mail server are generating substantial complaints from Yahoo users.
> - The message contained objectionable content or exhibited characteristics indicative of spam.
> - The IP (`x.x.x.x` or its subnet, i.e., .255 ) has a poor reputation.
> - We are seeing unusual traffic patterns from your mail servers.

Source: [SMTP Error Codes](https://senders.yahooinc.com/smtp-error-codes/), Yahoo Sender Hub. Checked October 2026.

## Why it happens

- Yahoo users mark your mail as spam. Yahoo asks every sender to keep the spam rate below 0.3%
  ([Sender Requirements & Recommendations](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/), checked October 2026).
- Volume jumps, for example the first large campaign from a new IP address or sending provider.
- The sending IP address, or its range, has a poor reputation.
- The content looks like spam.

## How ReputeCast classifies it

- While the sending provider retries, each refused attempt is a [deferral](/reference/glossary/#deferral): a
  <UiPath path="providers.events.optionDeferred" /> event in the event log. Deferrals don't appear in the campaign
  report's bounces table.
- Every workspace starts with a [reply rule](/reference/glossary/#reply-rule) for this code: when 10 Yahoo replies
  contain `TSS04` within 10 minutes, ReputeCast pauses Yahoo for 2 hours on every route, before any rate threshold. A
  second default rule does the same for the older `TS04`. The rest of the send keeps going, and Yahoo resumes by itself
  after the 2 hours. Change or turn off the rules in [traffic rules](/providers/traffic-rules/).
- Deferrals and soft bounces also count toward Yahoo's deferral thresholds, each message once, over the last 10 minutes.
  At 15% of the mail sent to Yahoo, ReputeCast slows Yahoo to half rate; at 40% it
  [auto-pauses](/reference/glossary/#auto-pause) the Yahoo share of the campaign, or the whole campaign when the pause
  scope is set to the campaign. Each level waits for 500 messages to Yahoo in that time and at least 5 deferrals.
- If the sending provider stops retrying, the [bounce class](/reference/glossary/#bounce-class) is `reputation`, from
  the `TSS04` tag, and the report row is <UiPath path="reports.bounceType.blocked" />. A block for the sender's
  reputation never suppresses the address, even when the sending provider calls it permanent (see
  [when ReputeCast suppresses an address](/smtp-errors/#when-reputecast-suppresses-an-address)).
- When a sending provider passes only `421 4.7.0` without the tag, the class is `temporary`
  (<UiPath path="reports.bounceType.deferralExpired" />).

## Before you begin

- `providers:view` to read the event log.
- `providers:manage` to change the throttles in <UiPath path="nav.settings > settings.providers" /> and the reply rules
  in <UiPath path="nav.providers > providers.tabTraffic" />.
- `campaigns:send` to resume an auto-paused send or change its rates.

## Bring the deferrals down

<Steps>

1. Confirm the code. Open <UiPath path="nav.providers > providers.tabEvents" />, set
   <UiPath path="providers.events.typeFilter" /> to <UiPath path="providers.events.optionDeferred" /> and search for a
   Yahoo recipient. <UiPath path="providers.events.colCode" /> shows the `[TSS04]` tag and the IP address Yahoo
   deferred.
2. Slow down to Yahoo. By default ReputeCast throttles Yahoo to 4 messages per second on every route; lower it in
   <UiPath path="nav.settings > settings.providers > senders.providers.throttlesTitle" />. During a send, lower the Yahoo
   rate with <UiPath path="live.adjustRates" /> on the [live monitor](/campaigns/live-monitor/). After an auto-pause,
   the live monitor recommends a lower rate for the Yahoo share; resume with that change.
3. Bring complaints down. Send only to contacts who asked for your mail, exclude those who stopped opening
   ([keep your list healthy](/audience/list-health/)), and keep
   [one-click unsubscribe](/deliverability/one-click-unsubscribe/) working so readers leave instead of reporting spam.
4. If the IP address or sending provider is new, follow a [warm-up plan](/providers/warm-up/).
5. If the deferrals go on for a long time, Yahoo's Sender Hub suggests a
   [Sender Support Request](https://senders.yahooinc.com/contact/#sender-support-request) with the error and diagnostic
   codes from your logs. The event log's CSV export has them.

</Steps>

## Check that it worked

- The live monitor's Yahoo deferral rate stays low during the next send, and its
  <UiPath path="live.model.thresholdReplies" /> row shows the `TSS04` rule well under its count.
- The event log shows delivered events for Yahoo recipients instead of new `[TSS04]` deferrals.

## Troubleshooting

- **The send keeps pausing on Yahoo.** When the `TSS04` rule paused it, let Yahoo resume by itself after 2 hours, or
  resume it at a lower rate with <UiPath path="live.adjustRates" />. Lower the Yahoo throttle for future sends.
- **Yahoo complaints are high.** Read [bounces, complaints and provider limits](/deliverability/bounces-and-complaints/);
  by default a running send auto-pauses Yahoo when its complaint rate passes 0.10%.
- **You send from a shared IP address.** Yahoo's Sender Hub notes that on a shared IP address, mail from other domains
  can hurt your IP reputation, and suggests asking your provider for dedicated IP addresses. A new dedicated IP address
  needs a warm-up plan.
