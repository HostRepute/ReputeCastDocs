Gmail 550 5.1.1 is a permanent refusal: the Gmail account you sent to doesn't exist. ReputeCast takes the address off
every future send by itself, so there is nothing to fix for that address; the work is keeping new invalid addresses out
of your list.

## What Gmail means

> The email account that you tried to reach does not exist. Please double-check the recipient's email address for typos
> or unnecessary spaces.

Source: [Gmail SMTP errors and codes](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/support/troubleshooting/gmail-smtp-errors-and-codes),
Google Workspace Help. Checked October 2026.

## Why it happens

- A typo when the contact signed up (`gmial.com`, a missing letter).
- The account was deleted after the contact subscribed. Old lists collect these over time.
- Fake or mistyped addresses in an imported list.
- Bots that fill in signup forms with made-up addresses.

## How ReputeCast classifies it

- [Bounce class](/reference/glossary/#bounce-class): `mailbox_unknown`, from the enhanced status code 5.1.1.
- Report row: <UiPath path="reports.bounceType.unknownUser" />, with <UiPath path="reports.autoSuppressed" /> in the
  <UiPath path="reports.colSuppression" /> column.
- Sending providers report it as a [hard bounce](/reference/glossary/#hard-bounce). ReputeCast adds the address to the
  shared [suppression list](/audience/suppressions/) for every stream and every sending provider, and marks the contact
  as bounced.
- Hard and soft bounces count toward the bounce rate of a running send. Above 2.0% by default, ReputeCast auto-pauses
  the share of the affected mailbox provider or sending provider, or the whole campaign; see
  [monitor a send and auto-pause](/campaigns/live-monitor/).
- Unknown users also have their own threshold over the whole send, at two levels: the first warns the people who can
  send campaigns, the second pauses the send. The [auto-pause defaults](/campaigns/live-monitor/#defaults-and-limits)
  list both levels, and [traffic rules](/providers/traffic-rules/) change them and their actions.

## Before you begin

- `reports:view` to read the campaign report.
- `subscribers:view` to look at the contacts that bounced.
- `subscribers:verify` (sensitive) to run address verification, if you use it.

## Keep invalid addresses off your list

<Steps>

1. See how many addresses bounced. Open <UiPath path="nav.campaigns" /> and the campaign's
   <UiPath path="campaigns.report" />. The <UiPath path="reports.bounceType.unknownUser" /> row shows the count and each
   sending provider's original code.
2. Find where they came from. [Growth sources](/audience/growth-sources/) show the bounce rate of each way contacts
   joined and flag a source whose bounces run at more than twice the workspace's rate.
3. Verify addresses before you mail an old list. [Address verification](/audience/verification/) checks a list in bulk,
   in the import preview, or on your signup forms as people type.
4. Confirm new signups with [double opt-in](/audience/lists-and-consent/), so a mistyped address never becomes a
   subscribed contact.
5. Protect your [signup forms](/forms/signup-forms/) from bots; the built-in spam protection is on by default.

</Steps>

## Check that it worked

- The next campaigns' <UiPath path="reports.bounceType.unknownUser" /> rows get smaller.
- The bounced addresses are in <UiPath path="nav.audience > audience.tabSuppressions" /> with their original code, and
  Send Readiness counts them as excluded from the next campaign's audience.

## Troubleshooting

- **An address you know works was suppressed.** Gmail said the account didn't exist when the message arrived. Check the
  original code in <UiPath path="nav.audience > audience.tabSuppressions" />, column
  <UiPath path="audience.suppressions.colReason" />. If you're sure it was a mistake, remove it with
  <UiPath path="audience.suppressions.removeButton" />.
- **Many 550 5.1.1 bounces right after an import.** The list is old. Pause sending to it, verify it, and send to the
  verified part first.
- **Amazon SES review warnings.** A high hard-bounce rate can put an SES account
  [under review](/smtp-errors/amazon-ses-under-review/).

<Aside type="caution">
Removing an address that hard-bounced puts its contact back to pending consent. It gets mail again only after a new
opt-in.
</Aside>
