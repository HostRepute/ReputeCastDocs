An Amazon SES account under review is not an SMTP error: it is AWS telling you that the mail your account sends has an
issue, usually too many bounces or complaints, and that you have a review period to fix it. Your mail still goes out
during the review. If the issue isn't fixed in time, AWS can pause the account's sending.

## What AWS means

AWS describes the status on its reputation metrics page:

> **Under review** – Your account is under review. If the issues that caused us to place your account under review
> aren't resolved by the end of the review period, we might pause your account's ability to send email.

> **Sending paused** – We've paused your account's ability to send email. While your account's ability to send email
> is paused, you won't be able to send email using Amazon SES.

For bounces and complaints AWS publishes the levels:

| Rate           | Account placed under review | Sending might be paused |
| -------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Bounce rate    | 5% or greater               | 10% or greater          |
| Complaint rate | 0.1% or greater             | 0.5% or greater         |

AWS calculates both over a representative volume of your mail, not a fixed period, and its bounce rate counts only hard
bounces to domains you haven't verified. Other causes include reports from anti-spam organizations, spam traps,
listbombing and links to blocklisted domains.

Sources: [Reputation metrics messages](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/reputationdashboardmessages.html) and
[Amazon SES sending review process FAQs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/faqs-enforcement.html), Amazon SES
Developer Guide. Checked October 2026.

## Where ReputeCast shows it

ReputeCast reads the account's enforcement status from the SES API (`GetAccount`, field `EnforcementStatus`) in its
provider health check, every 5 minutes:

| SES status   | <UiPath path="providers.overview.factAccountStatus" /> in ReputeCast |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `HEALTHY`    | `Production ·` <UiPath path="ses.enforcementNone" />                 |
| `PROBATION`  | `Production ·` <UiPath path="ses.enforcementProbation" />            |
| `SHUTDOWN`   | `Production ·` <UiPath path="ses.enforcementShutdown" />             |

- <UiPath path="nav.providers" /> shows it on the SES card, under <UiPath path="providers.overview.factAccountStatus" />.
- <UiPath path="nav.deliverability > placement.health" />, in <UiPath path="deliverability.providerHealthTitle" />,
  adds it to the SES badge and draws the SES bounce and complaint rates against the same review and pause levels.
- [Send Readiness](/campaigns/send-readiness/) has a check per sending provider in its
  <UiPath path="review.groupReputation" /> group. For SES it warns when the bounce or complaint rate is close to or over
  the review level, and blocks the send at the pause level (10% bounces or 0.5% complaints).

ReputeCast's rates cover the last 7 days of mail it sent through that SES account. AWS uses its own representative
volume, so the numbers can differ from the SES console.

When AWS pauses sending, SES refuses every send. ReputeCast marks the provider down once half of its calls fail over 3
minutes (at least 5 calls) and handles it as an outage; see
[provider health and outages](/providers/health-and-outages/).

## Before you begin

- `providers:view` and `deliverability:view` to see the status and the rates.
- `providers:manage` to change routes.
- Access to the AWS account, to reply to the review case in AWS Support Center.

## Respond to the review

<Steps>

1. Read the notification AWS sent to the email address of your AWS account, and the case it opened in AWS Support
   Center. It names the issue, for example bounces or complaints.
2. Find the cause in ReputeCast. On <UiPath path="nav.deliverability > placement.health" />, compare the SES rates with
   the review levels. In recent campaign reports, look at the <UiPath path="reports.bounceType.unknownUser" /> row and
   the complaints, and note which lists or imports they came from.
3. Lower the risk while you work. AWS advises you to stop sending until the problem is fixed if you can. In ReputeCast,
   don't schedule new campaigns to the affected lists, or move Broadcasts to another sending provider with a
   [route](/providers/routing/) so that only System and Transactional mail keeps using SES.
4. Fix the cause. For bounces, verify old lists before you mail them ([verify email addresses](/audience/verification/))
   and let the [list health tiers](/audience/list-health/) stop mail to inactive contacts. For complaints, send only to
   contacts with consent and keep [one-click unsubscribe](/deliverability/one-click-unsubscribe/) in every message. For
   listbombing, check the spam protection of your [signup forms](/forms/signup-forms/).
5. Reply to the AWS case. AWS asks for the root cause, the changes you have already made, and how they prevent the
   issue from happening again.

</Steps>

## Check that it worked

- The SES card's <UiPath path="providers.overview.factAccountStatus" /> goes back to `Production ·`
  <UiPath path="ses.enforcementNone" />.
- Send Readiness's SES check in <UiPath path="review.groupReputation" /> passes.

## Troubleshooting

- **The status shows `sending paused by AWS`.** Sending through SES has stopped. Route the mail to another sending
  provider, finish the steps above and request a review in the AWS case.
- **ReputeCast's rates look fine, but SES is under review.** AWS counts a longer representative volume and other
  signals, such as spam traps or reports from anti-spam organizations. Read the AWS notification for the reason.
- **The account is still in the sandbox.** That is a different status; see
  [move Amazon SES out of the sandbox](/deliverability/amazon-ses-production-access/).
