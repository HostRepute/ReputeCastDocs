ReputeCast gives you the role-based access control email marketing teams need: every member of a workspace has one
[role](/reference/glossary/#role), and a role is a set of [permissions](/reference/glossary/#permission) such as
`campaigns:send`. API keys and MCP agents hold permissions too, and never more than the member who created them.

## How it works

1. **A role decides what a member can do.** Each member has exactly one role per workspace. Four roles are built in:
   Owner, Admin, Editor and Viewer. They are read-only and get new permissions automatically when you upgrade.
2. **A permission includes its view permission.** Giving `campaigns:send` also gives `campaigns:view`, and
   `team:manage` includes `team:invite`. The role editor shows these as "included by".
3. **A scope narrows where a member works.** A scope is set on the membership (when you invite or change a member),
   not on the role. A scoped member sees and acts only on the chosen lists, and picks only the chosen sender domains in
   the campaign composer. Their email, SMS, push and WhatsApp sends go only to those lists: the composer offers them no
   segments, and a send checks its audience against the scope of whoever sends it. They can't download the
   whole-workspace export either. An empty scope means everything. See
   [what a scope reaches](#what-a-scope-reaches).
4. **Changes apply at once.** Editing a role or a member's role applies on their next request. API keys and MCP agents
   follow their creator: a key's permissions are its own permissions, cut down to what its creator holds right now,
   and its scope is the narrower of its own and its creator's current one.
5. **Every decision is logged.** Role changes, refused grants and denied actions on sensitive permissions are written
   to the [audit log](/team/audit-log/).

## Built-in roles

| Role   | What it is for                                                                                         |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Owner  | Everything, including transferring ownership and deleting the workspace. At least one per workspace.   |
| Admin  | Everything except transferring ownership and deleting the workspace.                                    |
| Editor | Creates and sends: campaigns, templates, automations, segments without SQL, forms, imports, website and products. Handles replies, discount codes and comments on posts. No approving, exporting contacts, deleting contacts, providers, team or keys. |
| Viewer | Read-only. Never sees secrets, and doesn't read replies (`replies:view`).                               |

Client workspaces of an [agency](/team/agencies/) add two more built-in roles, Client approver and Client viewer.
The matrix lists which permission each built-in workspace role includes; every key is described in
[permissions and roles](/reference/permissions/).

### Replies, discount codes and comments

Four permissions cover the [replies inbox](/audience/replies/), [discount codes](/campaigns/discount-codes/) and
[comments on posts](/forms/post-comments/). Owners, Admins and Editors hold all four; Viewers hold none.
Available from the next release after 1.6.0.

| Permission          | Group             | Allows                                                                                  |
| ------------------- | ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `replies:view`      | Contacts          | Reading the replies inbox and the other mail ReputeCast receives.                       |
| `replies:manage`    | Contacts          | Marking replies done or open, and unsubscribing a contact from a reply. Includes `replies:view`. |
| `coupons:manage` ⚑  | Campaigns         | Creating, changing and archiving pools, and uploading, creating, removing or exporting their codes. Seeing pools needs `campaigns:view`. |
| `comments:moderate` | Website and pages | Publishing, hiding, deleting and pinning comments, replying as the publication and blocking commenters. |

MCP agents can't hold any of them, and their actions, apart from reading replies, are for signed-in people only.

## Custom roles

Choose <UiPath path="roles.duplicateToCustomize" /> on a built-in role, or <UiPath path="roles.newRole" />, to start a
custom role. In the editor, <UiPath path="roles.startFromEditor" /> picks the Editor permissions you hold. There is no
limit on the number of custom roles; names are up to 60 characters, unique, and can't reuse a built-in name.

The <UiPath path="roles.impactTitle" /> card updates after each change: how many members, API keys and MCP agents are
affected, which sensitive permissions the role holds and what it means for approvals. When you save changes to a role
that people hold, a dialog lists who loses or gains what before anything changes. A role that members or pending
invitations still use can't be deleted; move them to another role first.

## The escalation guard

Nobody can grant a permission they don't hold: not to a role, a member, an invitation, an API key or an MCP agent.

- Permissions you don't hold are locked in the role editor, with the names of up to two people who can grant them.
- Saving a role with a permission you lack answers `403` with the code `escalation_denied`.
- You can only invite or assign a role whose permissions you all hold, and a scope within your own.
- You can't change, deactivate or remove a member whose role holds a permission you don't, so an Admin can't touch an
  Owner.
- Only a transfer of ownership makes someone an Owner, and the new Owner needs two-factor authentication.

## Sensitive permissions and two-factor authentication

Permissions that send, approve, publish, export, delete contacts or manage providers, the team, keys, webhooks,
agents and discount codes are [sensitive](/reference/glossary/#sensitive-permission) (⚑). `webhooks:manage` is
sensitive since 1.5.0: an endpoint sends what its creator can see out of the workspace for as long as it runs. By
default, the instance requires two-factor authentication for everyone who holds one in any workspace, with a 7-day
grace period. Editors hold sensitive permissions such as `campaigns:send`, so they are covered by it.

When the grace period ends, the person can still sign in but can't use the app or the API until they add an
authenticator app or a passkey. Members who sign in with single sign-on are covered when their identity provider
enforces two-factor. Instance admins choose the policy in
<UiPath path="shell.userMenuInstanceAdmin > admin.authentication > admin.twoFactorEnforcement" />.

## Who can hold a permission

| Rule         | Permissions                                              | Why                                                                 |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| People only  | `integrations:manage`, `ai:use`                          | API keys and MCP agents can't hold them.                            |
| No agents    | `subscribers:verify`, `placement:run`, `placement:manage`, `replies:view`, `replies:manage`, `coupons:manage`, `comments:moderate` | They send addresses or copies out, reach seed mailboxes, or handle what readers wrote and the codes they get. Keys may hold them. |
| Owner only   | `workspace:transfer`, `workspace:delete`                 | Only the built-in Owner role holds them; custom roles can't.       |

## What a scope reaches

A member, API key or MCP agent limited to some lists or sender domains stays within them wherever ReputeCast reads
contacts or sends mail on their behalf. Since 1.5.0 this covers:

- **Every send path.** <UiPath path="composer.reviewSend" /> (send, schedule, schedule as approved), moving a
  scheduled email, the live monitor's resume, retry, move, send again and continue after a canary, and MCP's
  `resume_campaign` all check the campaign as it is against the caller's lists and sender domains. Moving an SMS, push
  or WhatsApp send, or resuming a paused SMS send, does too. Stopping a send never needs a scope.
- **Senders.** A new draft, a copy and a website post's campaign start from a sender within the author's sender
  domains.
- **Automations and digests.** A caller limited to lists publishes or resumes a flow only when every trigger is
  <UiPath path="automations.graph.kindJoinedList" /> on one of their lists; one limited to sender domains, only when
  the flow's emails go out from one of their domains. Setting up, turning on and releasing a digest is checked like a
  campaign's send.
- **Reads that name contacts.** The contacts at a flow's step, MCP's contact tools, and poll and survey exports leave
  out contacts that aren't on the caller's lists. Previewing an automation email as a contact needs
  `subscribers:view` and a contact on those lists.
- **Webhooks.** Subscribing an endpoint to an event needs the view permission of the data it carries, and events
  that carry contacts are refused to anyone limited to some lists. An endpoint sends for the person who last set its
  events or resumed it, and pauses, with the reason, when that person leaves, loses the permission or is limited to
  some lists. See [receive webhooks](/developers/webhooks/).

## When your role can't do something

A refused action says what your role can't do and who can help, never a permission key, for example "Sending needs a
permission you don't have. Ask Marcus Chen or Linh Nguyen." It names up to two members who hold the permission, Owners
first, then Admins. The same goes for the API's `permission_denied` problem: its text names who can help, and API
clients read the missing key from its `permission` field; see [API errors](/developers/api-errors/).

The keys stay where you set or audit them: API key scopes, MCP agent permissions, integration key scopes, the role
editor and the [audit log](/team/audit-log/).

## Asking for access

When a page answers that your role can't open it, choose <UiPath path="shell.stateRequestAccess" />. ReputeCast
suggests the smallest built-in role that has the missing permission and notifies everyone with `team:manage`. They
grant it by changing your role on the [Team page](/team/members/).

<Aside type="note">
  ReputeCast does not support SAML or SCIM. Map identity-provider groups to roles with
  [single sign-on with OIDC](/self-hosting/sso/).
</Aside>

## When to change them

- Create a custom role when a built-in role gives too much, for example writers who edit but must request approval to
  send.
- Use a scope rather than a new role when people do the same work on different lists or brands.
- Ask an instance admin to require two-factor authentication for everyone when every member should use a second factor.
