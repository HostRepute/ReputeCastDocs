ReputeCast is an open-source, self-hosted newsletter platform for newsletters, email marketing and product email. You run
it on your own server, it sends through the [sending provider](/reference/glossary/#sending-provider) you already trust,
and it checks whether your mail will reach the inbox before you send, then guards your
[sender reputation](/reference/glossary/#sender-reputation) while it sends.

It is built by [HostRepute](https://hostrepute.com), an email-reputation monitoring service, and it works with or without
a HostRepute account.

## Before, while and after you send

ReputeCast acts at three points of every send:

1. **Before you send.** [Send Readiness](/reference/glossary/#send-readiness) checks every campaign and gives a verdict:
   Ready, Ready with warnings or Blocked. The checks cover sender reputation and blocklists (the domains of your links
   are looked up live), SPF, DKIM and DMARC, content and every A/B variant, Liquid, languages, accessibility, links,
   compliance (such as one-click unsubscribe and the footer address), your workspace's own
   [sending rules](/campaigns/sending-rules/), audience quality and routing, with estimates that follow the sending
   engine's own rules. Each finding comes with its evidence and, where possible, a
   [one-click fix](/reference/glossary/#one-click-fix) you can undo. A scheduled send is checked again when it starts.
   Read [Send Readiness: check before you send](/campaigns/send-readiness/).
2. **While it sends.** [Guarded sending](/reference/glossary/#guarded-sending) shows delivered, bounced, complained,
   opened and clicked counts per mailbox provider (Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, iCloud) and per sending provider, with an ETA
   for each. When bounces (by type), deferrals or complaints (only where mailbox providers report them) cross your
   thresholds, or a mailbox provider's replies match your [traffic rules](/providers/traffic-rules/), ReputeCast slows
   down or pauses the affected mailbox provider, route or whole campaign and explains what tripped. A
   [canary](/reference/glossary/#canary) phase tests new domains and unusual audiences first, and a message is never
   sent twice when a provider's answer is lost. Read [Monitor a send and auto-pause](/campaigns/live-monitor/).
3. **After it sends.** Reports count human, privacy-protected (Apple Mail) and machine opens apart, with the open rate
   of the opens you can measure. Clicks are settled against security scanners before they count, complaint rates cover
   only the mailbox providers that report complaints, and A/B winners are decided by sample size and significance. Each
   number is compared with your last issues, each bounce keeps its original code from the provider, and results are
   split per mailbox provider and per sending provider. The Deliverability area tracks the health of your domains, IP
   addresses and provider accounts. Read [Read campaign reports](/campaigns/reports/) and
   [Email deliverability for self-hosted senders](/deliverability/overview/).

## Any sending provider, several at once

ReputeCast has built-in adapters for Amazon SES, Postmark, Mailgun, SendGrid, Resend, SparkPost, Mailjet, Brevo, Azure
Communication Services, your own Postal server and any SMTP server, plus a generic HTTP adapter for other email APIs and
[community adapters](/adapters/overview/) that run in their own process. Compare them in
[Choose a sending provider](/providers/choose/).

You can connect several providers at once. A [route](/reference/glossary/#route) decides which provider delivers each
email, by kind of mail, list, sender domain or the recipient's mailbox provider, which ReputeCast finds from the MX
records of the address's domain (so Google Workspace counts as Gmail). Weighted splits are sticky, failover goes only to
a provider that passes its checks, and [warm-up](/providers/warm-up/) is enforced as mail goes out. Every provider's
bounces and complaints land in one [suppression list](/reference/glossary/#suppression-list), so a bounce at one
provider stops mail to that address everywhere. Read [How ReputeCast works](/getting-started/concepts/).

## Who it is for

- **Publishers and creators** who want their newsletter, website and paid subscriptions on infrastructure they control.
- **SaaS founders** who send product updates, onboarding automations and transactional email from one place.
- **Marketing teams** leaving a hosted email platform who want to keep their consent records and choose their own
  sending provider.
- **Agencies** that run client workspaces with approvals and per-client usage.

You run ReputeCast yourself, so someone on the team needs to be comfortable with Docker or a Linux server. Start with the
[quickstart](/getting-started/quickstart/), or read the [system requirements](/self-hosting/requirements/).

## HostRepute is optional

Every check in ReputeCast works without a HostRepute account. If you [connect HostRepute](/deliverability/hostrepute/),
it adds more blocklist sources, Google Postmaster Tools, Microsoft SNDS data, DMARC reports and alerts. ReputeCast sends it
only your sending domains, your dedicated IP addresses and any server you add to monitoring; it never sends contacts,
email content or campaign statistics.

## How it is built

<ArchitectureDiagram />

One `reputecast` binary serves the web app, the REST API, public pages, provider webhooks and the MCP server, runs
background jobs in PostgreSQL, and hands mail to your sending providers.

The binary runs on Linux, macOS and Windows, or as a Docker image. PostgreSQL is the only other service it needs. For
larger installations you can run the web, ingest and worker roles as separate processes; see
[Scale with web, ingest and worker roles](/self-hosting/scaling/).
