This page explains the newsletter sending architecture behind ReputeCast: who works where, what kinds of mail exist, how
each email finds its sending provider, and how delivery results come back and protect your next send. The terms used here
appear throughout the app and the rest of these docs.

## Instances, workspaces and members

- An [instance](/reference/glossary/#instance) is one ReputeCast installation: the server, its PostgreSQL database and
  every workspace on it. [Instance admins](/reference/glossary/#instance-admin) manage users, sign-in methods, single
  sign-on and updates for the whole instance.
- A [workspace](/reference/glossary/#workspace) is a separate space with its own lists, campaigns, automations, sending
  providers and team. An instance can have any number of workspaces, for example one per publication, brand or client.
- A [member](/reference/glossary/#member) is a person in a workspace, with exactly one [role](/reference/glossary/#role)
  there. The built-in roles are Owner, Admin, Editor and Viewer; you can add custom roles with your own set of
  permissions. API keys and AI agents get permissions the same way and can never hold more than the person who created
  them. Read [Roles and permissions](/team/roles-and-permissions/).

## Sending providers and mailbox providers

ReputeCast always says which of the two it means:

- A [sending provider](/reference/glossary/#sending-provider) delivers your mail: Amazon SES, Postmark, Mailgun, a Postal
  server, any SMTP server and the others in [Choose a sending provider](/providers/choose/).
- A [mailbox provider](/reference/glossary/#mailbox-provider) is where your reader opens the mail: Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo,
  iCloud and others.

ReputeCast finds each contact's mailbox provider from the MX records of the address's domain, so a company on Google
Workspace counts as Gmail and one on Microsoft 365 as Outlook. Routing, Send Readiness, reports and warm-up all read
that answer. Gmail and iCloud never report complaints per message, so their complaint rates read n/a and stay out of
complaint-rate totals.

Reports and the live monitor show results per mailbox provider and per sending provider side by side, because a problem
can sit on either side.

## Four streams

A [stream](/reference/glossary/#stream) is a kind of mail. Each stream has its own route and failover policy, so a bad
campaign never blocks a password reset.

| Stream        | What it carries                                                                                       |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Broadcasts    | Campaigns.                                                                                            |
| Automations   | Emails sent by automation flows.                                                                      |
| System        | Double opt-in confirmations, preference-center links, team invitations and password resets.          |
| Transactional | Product email your own application sends through the transactional API or the SMTP relay.             |

Invitations, password resets and email notifications work only once a sending provider carries the System stream.
Transactional mail goes first when it shares a sending provider with Broadcasts. Read
[Transactional email in ReputeCast](/transactional/overview/).

## Routes, weighted splits and failover

A [route](/reference/glossary/#route) decides which sending provider delivers an email. Each stream has a default rule,
and you can add rules for particular lists, sender domains or the recipient's mailbox provider. A rule can send all its
mail through one provider or share it as a [weighted split](/reference/glossary/#weighted-split), such as 90/10 while
you move to a new provider or warm up a new IP address.

When a provider fails, the stream's [failover](/reference/glossary/#failover) policy decides what happens:

- **Automatic**: mail moves to a fallback provider that passes its checks, such as being available, allowing that kind
  of mail and having a verified sender domain. This is the default for Automations, System and Transactional.
- **Pause and ask**: the remaining recipients wait until a person decides. This is the default for Broadcasts, because
  moving a large send to a cold provider in the middle of it can hurt your reputation.
- **Automatic, warmed-up only**: mail moves only to a fallback that is not warming up; otherwise ReputeCast pauses and
  asks.

Read [Route mail across providers](/providers/routing/).

## One event model and one suppression list

Every sending provider reports deliveries, bounces and complaints in its own format. ReputeCast receives these reports
on its public URL (`/hooks/<adapter>/<token>`), checks the signature, stores the raw payload and answers at once; a
background job then translates each report into a [normalized event](/reference/glossary/#normalized-event):
delivered, deferred, hard bounce, soft bounce, complaint, rejected, open, click or unsubscribe. The provider's original
code stays with the event. SMTP servers have no webhooks, so ReputeCast reads their bounces from a bounce mailbox
instead.

Hard bounces, complaints and unsubscribes go on one [suppression list](/reference/glossary/#suppression-list) shared by
every sending provider: a bounce at one provider stops mail to that address everywhere. Each entry applies to all mail or
to marketing mail only, and only hard bounces and complaints that apply to all mail are pushed to the providers. Read [Delivery events and suppression sync](/providers/events-and-sync/) and
[The suppression list](/audience/suppressions/).

## Consent

ReputeCast records [consent](/reference/glossary/#consent) per list, with its evidence: how and when the contact
subscribed and the exact text they agreed to. Signup forms can use [double opt-in](/reference/glossary/#double-opt-in),
and imports keep the consent status from the platform you leave: unsubscribed, bounced and complained contacts become
suppressions and are never subscribed again. Read [Lists, consent and double opt-in](/audience/lists-and-consent/).

## Guarded sending from start to finish

<SendFlowDiagram />

Send Readiness checks a campaign before it starts, routing hands each recipient to a sending provider, delivery events
come back through webhooks, and thresholds decide whether the send keeps going or pauses.

1. **Send Readiness.** [Send Readiness](/reference/glossary/#send-readiness) checks reputation, authentication, content,
   languages, accessibility, conditional content, links, compliance, audience quality and routing, and gives a verdict:
   Ready, Ready with warnings or Blocked. It runs again 15 minutes before a scheduled send and when the send starts. A
   Block stops the send; new warnings, an audience that grew by more than 10% or edits after scheduling hold it until a
   person looks again.
2. **Routing.** The send builds its recipient list in pages and routes each recipient. Batches go to each provider
   within its rate limit and each mailbox provider's throttle, and [warm-up](/providers/warm-up/) caps hold what a young
   provider can't take yet. Every message is claimed before it goes out, so nothing is sent twice. A send can start with
   a [canary](/reference/glossary/#canary) phase: the most engaged few percent and your seed mailboxes get it first.
3. **Delivery events.** Providers report deliveries, bounces and complaints, and ReputeCast normalizes them.
4. **Thresholds.** During the send, ReputeCast checks bounce, complaint and deferral rates every 2 seconds. A rate over
   its threshold, with enough evidence, [auto-pauses](/reference/glossary/#auto-pause) only the mailbox provider or
   sending provider it concerns, or the whole campaign if you chose that scope. Deferrals slow a send down before they
   pause it, complaints count only where mailbox providers report them, and mailbox provider reply rules can slow one
   mailbox provider. A complaint watch follows the send for 72 hours.
5. **Decision.** The live monitor explains what tripped. A person with permission resumes, resumes with changes or
   cancels the send.

## When to change them

- **Lower the thresholds** for a list you have not mailed in a while, or for a new sending domain, so a bad segment pauses
  sooner.
- **Raise them slightly** only when a known cause inflates a rate, such as a list you know contains old addresses that
  you are cleaning up on purpose.
- **Pause the whole campaign** instead of one route when any problem should stop everything, for example an
  announcement that must reach everyone or no one.

Change the workspace defaults in <UiPath path="nav.settings > settings.providers" />, the thresholds by bounce type and
the reply rules in <UiPath path="nav.providers > providers.tabTraffic" />, and a single campaign's values in
<UiPath path="composer.reviewSend" />; read [Workspace sending defaults](/providers/workspace-defaults/),
[Traffic rules](/providers/traffic-rules/) and [Monitor a send and auto-pause](/campaigns/live-monitor/).
