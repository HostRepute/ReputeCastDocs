A new [dedicated IP](/reference/glossary/#dedicated-ip), a new sending provider or your own Postal server has no sending
history, so mailbox providers treat a sudden flood of mail from it with suspicion. A
[warm-up](/reference/glossary/#warm-up) plan gives it an IP warm-up schedule: a daily cap that rises over a few weeks,
mail to your most engaged contacts first, and an automatic slowdown when bounces or complaints rise.

## Before you begin

- Connect the provider. Read [Connect a sending provider](/providers/connect/).
- For its share of Broadcasts to rise on schedule, put it in a weighted split on the Broadcasts route next to a provider
  that is already warm, for example 90% Amazon SES and 10% Postal. You set this up on the connect wizard's Streams step
  (role Weighted split). Read [Route mail across providers](/providers/routing/).
- You need the `providers:manage` permission. With `providers:view` you can follow a plan.
- Engagement tiers come from [list health](/audience/list-health/): Active, Cooling, At risk and Sunset.

## Start a plan

<Steps>

1. Open <UiPath path="nav.providers > providers.tabWarmup" /> and choose <UiPath path="providers.warmup.newPlan" />.

2. Pick the <UiPath path="providers.warmup.provider" />. Enter its <UiPath path="providers.warmup.dedicatedIp" /> if it
   has one; leave it empty to warm the provider's shared pool for your domain.

3. Choose <UiPath path="providers.warmup.startWarmup" />. The provider's status changes to Warming up · day 1 of N, its
   first day's cap is 200 emails, and its Broadcasts share starts at 10%. Each provider can have one plan at a time.

4. Adjust the <UiPath path="providers.warmup.settingsTitle" /> if you need to, then choose
   <UiPath path="providers.warmup.savePlan" />. Changes apply from tomorrow's cap.
   - <UiPath path="providers.warmup.length" />: 14 days (fast), 21 days (recommended for a new dedicated IP) or 30 days
     (cautious). A new length rebuilds the schedule between the same first and last volumes; days already sent keep
     their results.
   - <UiPath path="providers.warmup.ordering" />: <UiPath path="providers.warmup.orderEngaged" />, or
     <UiPath path="providers.warmup.orderRandom" />, which mails every engagement tier from the first day.
   - <UiPath path="providers.warmup.slowIfBounces" /> a bounce rate, or complaints exceed a complaint rate.
   - <UiPath path="providers.warmup.whenTrips" />: <UiPath path="providers.warmup.actionHold" />,
     <UiPath path="providers.warmup.actionStepBack" /> or <UiPath path="providers.warmup.actionPause" />.

</Steps>

## How the schedule works

1. **The ramp.** Day 1 is 200 emails. The cap grows by the same factor each day until the last day reaches the target:
   your largest campaign audience so far, or 48,000 when the workspace hasn't sent a campaign yet. The day's rate limit
   spreads the cap over about an hour (the cap divided by 3,000, per second), never goes below 1 email per second and
   never exceeds the provider's own maximum rate.
2. **Who gets mail first.** With engaged-first ordering, the first quarter of the days mails only Active contacts,
   Cooling contacts join until the halfway point, At risk contacts join after that, and the last day mails every tier.
3. **What happens to the rest.** For Broadcasts, a contact the plan doesn't allow today (the wrong tier, over today's
   cap, or the plan is paused) goes through the largest other share of the same route that isn't warming up, or else the
   route's fallback. Nobody is skipped because of the warm-up: if the route has no other provider, the mail still goes
   through the warming provider.
4. **The Broadcast share.** If the provider is in a Broadcasts weighted split with another provider, its share rises from
   10% to 15%, 25% and finally 40% over the plan; the share comes from the largest other provider in the split.
5. **The daily check.** ReputeCast checks every running plan each hour. When a day ends (in the workspace's time zone),
   it records what was sent, the bounce rate (bounces per message sent) and the complaint rate (complaints per message
   delivered). If either is over its guard, it holds the next day's cap, steps back one day or pauses the plan, as you
   chose, and writes it to the <UiPath path="providers.warmup.logTitle" />.
6. **The end.** After the last day, the plan is done, the provider's status returns to Healthy and it can take Broadcasts
   failover under the warmed-up-only policy.

<Aside type="note">
The plan settings also offer to spread each day's cap evenly across mailbox providers. This release saves that choice
with the plan, but sending doesn't apply it yet.
</Aside>

### Read the plan

The headline shows the day ("day 9 of 21"), when the plan finishes, today's cap and what was sent today, everything sent
during the warm-up against what was planned, the 7-day bounce and complaint rates beside their guards, the slowdowns so
far and today's rate limit. The <UiPath path="providers.warmup.rampTitle" /> chart and the table under it show each day's
cap, what was sent, its bounce and complaint rates, the tiers it mailed and its status: Done, Slowdown, Today or
Planned.

To stop the ramp for a while, choose <UiPath path="providers.warmup.pause" />. While the plan is paused, its Broadcast
share goes to the other providers of the route, and its cap stays where it is until you choose
<UiPath path="providers.warmup.resume" />. The days keep counting while it's paused, so the ramp continues from the day
you resume on.

## Check that it worked

- <UiPath path="nav.providers > providers.tabProviders" /> shows the provider as Warming up · day N of M, with today's
  warm-up cap.
- <UiPath path="nav.providers > providers.tabRouting" /> shows the planned share increases under the weighted split.
- After the first full day, the warm-up log has a "Day 1 complete" entry with what was sent and the bounce rate.

## Troubleshooting

- **"Every connected provider already has a warm-up plan"**: each provider has one plan at a time. Change the running
  plan's settings instead.
- **The Broadcast share doesn't rise**: the provider must be in the Broadcasts weighted split with at least one other
  provider whose share can give up the difference. A share you raised by hand above the planned one stays as it is.
- **A slowdown on the first days**: bounces on a new IP usually mean old or unverified addresses. Check
  [list health](/audience/list-health/) and the bounce codes in the [event log](/providers/events-and-sync/).
- **The plan paused itself**: the guard tripped and the plan is set to pause until you resume. Read the warm-up log for
  the day and the mailbox provider with most bounces, fix the cause, then choose
  <UiPath path="providers.warmup.resume" />.
