ReputeCast checks every connected sending provider in the background and gives it a status. When an email provider
outage starts, mail that must keep going out moves to a fallback that passes its checks, a campaign's share on the
failing provider waits for your decision, and traffic switches back once the provider has been healthy for 10 minutes.

carries, its sending IPs, its webhook freshness, its API error rate, quota and send rate, its cost this month and its
capabilities.

The bounce and complaint rates on a card cover the last 7 days (or are the provider's own figures, where it reports
them). They are shown against the provider account's limits, such as the Amazon SES review and pause levels, so you can
see how close your account is to them. You can change the limits shown in the provider's
<UiPath path="providers.overview.settings" />. They are for reference only: the thresholds that pause a send are the
[workspace sending defaults](/providers/workspace-defaults/) and the [traffic rules](/providers/traffic-rules/).

A rate without data is never shown as 0%:

- The complaint rate counts only mail delivered to [mailbox providers](/reference/glossary/#mailbox-provider) that
  report complaints. Gmail and iCloud never do, so a provider whose mail went only there reads "n/a" with the reason,
  "Gmail and iCloud don't report complaints".
- With nothing delivered in the last 7 days, the rates read "n/a" too.
- The API error rate of a provider that had no calls reads <UiPath path="providers.overview.errorRateNoCalls" />.

## How health is checked

1. Every 5 minutes, ReputeCast makes a read-only call to each provider (for example `GetAccount` for Amazon SES or
   `GET /server` for Postmark) and records the result, the quota and the send rate it reports. While a provider is
   Down or Degraded, it checks every minute.
2. Real sends count too: every API call during a send is recorded.
3. A provider is marked **Down** after 2 failed checks in a row, or when at least half of at least 5 calls in the last 3
   minutes failed. A `401` or similar "credentials rejected" answer marks it Down at once.
4. A provider is marked **Degraded** when its webhooks go silent, when it starts rejecting messages, or when ReputeCast
   can't read its bounce mailbox or event queue. Each of these opens an incident and notifies the people who manage
   sending providers.
5. A Down provider counts as recovered once calls succeed again (fewer than 1 in 10 failing) for 10 minutes. After you
   replace rejected credentials, it recovers at the next successful check.

The outage banner has a button to re-check the provider now instead of waiting for the next check.

## What happens during an outage

When a provider goes down, ReputeCast handles each stream that routes mail through it, following the stream's
[failover policy](/providers/routing/). Mail only moves to a fallback that passes every check first: available, its
terms allow bulk mail (for Broadcasts), out of sandbox, the sender's domain verified there, enough quota for the mail
that would move, and warm. A provider that is warming up takes failover mail only under the automatic policy and
within today's cap.

- **Automations, System and Transactional** switch to the stream's fallback automatically when it passes its checks.
  The banner says how many emails were rerouted. When the fallback fails a check, ReputeCast keeps retrying the
  provider instead of switching, and new mail for the stream waits until the provider recovers.
- **Broadcasts** follow the Broadcasts policy. With the default, pause and ask, each campaign sending through the
  provider has that provider's share held while the other shares keep sending, and the people who can send campaigns
  are asked to decide. With warmed-up-only or automatic failover, the share moves to the fallback when the policy
  allows it and the fallback passes its checks.

A held share goes through the sending engine's one outage path, whether the health check found the outage or the send
found it first: its calls failed for 3 minutes, it kept throttling the account, its API errors passed the send's limit,
or it refused the sender or ran out of quota. Until someone decides, the share tests the provider with one message a
minute and resumes by itself after 3 go through in a row. The [live monitor](/campaigns/live-monitor/) shows the same
hold with the engine's analysis and its choices.

For a held Broadcast share, the outage banner on the providers page offers three choices. Above them,
<UiPath path="providers.incidents.checksFirst" />, and each target lists the checks it passed.

- **Wait** for the provider: the share resumes on its own after the provider has been healthy for 10 minutes. It is
  recommended when no target passed every check.
- **Move** the held recipients to another provider. A target that failed a check can't be chosen. If it is still
  warming up and the move would exceed today's cap, the button says it isn't recommended.
- <UiPath path="providers.incidents.cancelSend" />: the held recipients don't get the campaign, the rest of the send
  stops, and what was already sent stays sent. This can't be undone.

Waiting and moving resume the send and need `campaigns:send`; cancelling needs `campaigns:pause`.

Members with `providers:manage` are notified when a provider goes down. Every step (down, failover, waiting, switched
back, recovered) is written to the <UiPath path="providers.incidents.timelineTitle" />, and the outage is recorded in the
[audit log](/team/audit-log/).

### Switching back

After 10 healthy minutes, streams that failed over return to their primary, and Broadcast shares set to wait resume
where they stopped.

### Silent webhooks

A provider's webhooks are silent when it keeps accepting mail but no delivery, bounce or complaint event comes back:
the messages it took are overdue past a grace period, as the "Webhooks silent" row under
[defaults and limits](#defaults-and-limits) sets out. A provider that has never reported an event is flagged as soon as
mail to it is overdue, so a webhook that never worked doesn't read "no events yet" forever. SMTP servers are exempt
because their bounces come from a mailbox.

While webhooks are silent, sending works but the status of new messages is unknown, so bounce protection for that route
is blind. The provider is marked Degraded. ReputeCast doesn't fail over on silence alone: Broadcasts on the provider wait
until events arrive or you decide, and other streams use their fallback when they have one. The banner shows what
ReputeCast checked (the API, whether the webhook is still registered at the provider, whether its webhook address is
public and when the provider last reached it), the likely causes and the impact: messages overdue and still inside the
30-minute grace, the campaigns affected and the bounces not yet suppressed. From there you can choose
<UiPath path="providers.incidents.sendTestEvent" /> (the provider sends one to the webhook, and its arrival proves the
address works), <UiPath path="providers.incidents.reregister" /> or, where the provider's API keeps past messages,
<UiPath path="providers.incidents.checkNow" /> to read the status of the overdue messages. The banner clears as soon as
events arrive. How events reach ReputeCast is in [delivery events and suppression sync](/providers/events-and-sync/).

### Rejecting messages

A provider that refuses messages it was handed, for example because the sender isn't verified there or the account is
suspended, is marked <UiPath path="providers.health.rejecting" /> once the refusals pass the "Rejecting messages" row
under [defaults and limits](#defaults-and-limits). The refused messages weren't sent. The incident shows
how many it refused and its last answer; check the sender's domain and the account at the provider. The status clears
once it accepts messages again. During a campaign, a provider that refuses the sender holds its share instead of failing
every message: nothing is marked failed, and the messages wait for you to fix the sender, move them or cancel them.

### Rejected credentials

When a provider answers that the credentials are no longer valid, for example because someone rotated the API key at the
provider, it is marked Down at once and its streams fail over as above. Replace the credentials in the provider's
<UiPath path="providers.overview.settings" />: ReputeCast checks the new ones with the provider before it stores them.

## When to change them

- **Give every stream a fallback that can take its mail.** A second provider with the sender's domain verified and
  enough quota turns an outage into a short failover for Automations, System and Transactional mail. Read
  [Route mail across providers](/providers/routing/).
- **Choose the Broadcasts policy deliberately.** Pause and ask is the safe default. Fail over automatically, only to a
  warmed-up provider, if you'd rather keep campaigns moving and your fallback has a sending history.
- **Make sure the webhooks can reach you.** Silent webhooks are usually a reverse proxy, a firewall or a public URL
  problem. Read [Reverse proxy and TLS](/self-hosting/reverse-proxy/) and
  [Troubleshooting](/self-hosting/troubleshooting/).
