ReputeCast checks every connected sending provider in the background and gives it a status. When an email provider
outage starts, mail that must keep going out moves to its fallback on its own, a campaign's share on the failing
provider waits for your decision, and traffic switches back once the provider has been healthy for 10 minutes.

## What the statuses mean

carries, its webhook freshness, its API error rate, quota and send rate, its cost this month and its capabilities.

The bounce and complaint rates on a card cover the last 7 days (or are the provider's own figures, where it reports
them). They are shown against the provider account's limits, such as the Amazon SES review and pause levels, so you can
see how close your account is to them. You can change the limits shown in the provider's
<UiPath path="providers.overview.settings" />. They are for reference only: the thresholds that pause a send are the
[workspace sending defaults](/providers/workspace-defaults/).

## How health is checked

1. Every 5 minutes, ReputeCast makes a read-only call to each provider (for example `GetAccount` for Amazon SES or
   `GET /server` for Postmark) and records the result, the quota and the send rate it reports. While a provider is
   Down or Degraded, it checks every minute.
2. Real sends count too: every API call during a send is recorded.
3. A provider is marked **Down** after 2 failed checks in a row, or when at least half of at least 5 calls in the last 3
   minutes failed. A `401` or similar "credentials rejected" answer marks it Down at once.
4. A provider is marked **Degraded** when it has accepted mail but reported no delivery, bounce or complaint event for 6
   hours. SMTP servers are exempt because their bounces come from a mailbox.
5. A Down provider counts as recovered once calls succeed again (fewer than 1 in 10 failing) for 10 minutes. After you
   replace rejected credentials, it recovers at the next successful check.

The outage banner has a button to re-check the provider now instead of waiting for the next check.

## What happens during an outage

When a provider goes down, ReputeCast handles each stream that routes mail through it, following the stream's
[failover policy](/providers/routing/):

- **Automations, System and Transactional** switch to the stream's fallback automatically, if the fallback is available.
  The banner says how many emails were rerouted. Without an available fallback, new mail for the stream waits until the
  provider recovers.
- **Broadcasts** follow the Broadcasts policy. With the default, pause and ask, each campaign that is sending through
  the provider has that provider's share held, while the other shares keep sending. Members who can send campaigns get
  a notification that the campaign is waiting for a decision. With warmed-up-only or automatic failover, the share moves
  to the fallback when the policy allows it.

For a held Broadcast share, the outage banner on the providers page offers three choices:

- **Wait** for the provider: the share resumes on its own after the provider has been healthy for 10 minutes.
- **Move** the held recipients to another provider you pick. If that provider is still warming up, ReputeCast warns you
  when the move would exceed its cap for today.
- <UiPath path="providers.incidents.cancelSend" />: the held recipients don't get the campaign. What was already sent
  stays sent. This can't be undone.

Waiting and moving resume the send and need `campaigns:send`; cancelling needs `campaigns:pause`.

A provider can also fail while a campaign is sending, before the next health check: the send holds that provider's share
after its calls have failed for 3 minutes (at least 5 calls), and the live monitor asks what to do with it. Read
[Monitor a send and auto-pause](/campaigns/live-monitor/).

Members with `providers:manage` are notified when a provider goes down. Every step (down, failover, waiting, switched
back, recovered) is written to the <UiPath path="providers.incidents.timelineTitle" />, and the outage is recorded in the
[audit log](/team/audit-log/).

### Switching back

After 10 healthy minutes, streams that failed over return to their primary, and Broadcast shares set to wait resume
where they stopped.

### Silent webhooks

While a provider is Degraded, sending works but the status of new messages is unknown, so bounce protection for that
route is blind. Until events arrive again, new mail is routed as if the provider were down: its share of Broadcasts is
held, and other streams use their fallback when they have one. The banner shows what ReputeCast checked (the
API, whether the webhook is still registered at the provider, whether ReputeCast's endpoint is reachable from the
internet), the likely causes and the messages still waiting for a status. From there you can choose
<UiPath path="providers.incidents.sendTestEvent" />, <UiPath path="providers.incidents.reregister" /> or, where the
provider's API keeps past events, backfill their status. The banner clears as soon as events arrive.

### Rejected credentials

When a provider answers that the credentials are no longer valid, for example because someone rotated the API key at the
provider, it is marked Down at once and its streams fail over as above. Replace the credentials in the provider's
<UiPath path="providers.overview.settings" />: ReputeCast checks the new ones with the provider before it stores them.

## When to change them

- **Give every stream a fallback.** A second provider turns an outage into a short failover for Automations, System and
  Transactional mail. Read [Route mail across providers](/providers/routing/).
- **Choose the Broadcasts policy deliberately.** Pause and ask is the safe default. Fail over automatically, only to a
  warmed-up provider, if you'd rather keep campaigns moving and your fallback has a sending history.
- **Make sure the webhooks can reach you.** Silent webhooks are usually a reverse proxy, a firewall or a public URL
  problem. Read [Reverse proxy and TLS](/self-hosting/reverse-proxy/) and
  [Troubleshooting](/self-hosting/troubleshooting/).
