Routing decides which [sending provider](/reference/glossary/#sending-provider) delivers each email: per stream, by list,
sender domain or the recipient's mailbox provider, with weighted splits and a fallback. This page sets up routes and
email failover multiple providers can take part in, then checks the result with the
[route tester](/reference/glossary/#route-tester) before you save.

## Before you begin

- Connect at least one provider; failover and splits need two. Read [Connect a sending provider](/providers/connect/).
- You need `providers:manage` to change routing. With `providers:view` you can open the page and use the route tester.

## How a route decides

Each email belongs to one of four [streams](/reference/glossary/#stream): Broadcasts (campaigns), Automations, System
(double opt-in confirmations, preference links, team invites) and Transactional (mail from your product). For every
recipient, ReputeCast:

1. Takes the stream's rules.
2. Checks the stream's conditions in order. The first one that matches decides: it sends to one provider, or through a
   weighted split of its own.
3. If no condition matches, uses the stream's default route: the primary provider, or a
   [sticky split](/reference/glossary/#sticky-split). Each provider of the split scores the address with a hash of the
   address and the provider, scaled by its weight, and the highest score wins. So the same recipient keeps the same
   provider from send to send, the order of the split changes nothing, and raising one provider's weight moves only
   about the difference to it; nobody else changes provider.
4. Checks the shared suppression list. A suppressed address gets nothing, whichever provider it was routed to.
5. If the provider is [warming up](/providers/warm-up/), applies its plan: the day's engagement tiers and cap, with
   what's over going through a warm provider of the route or waiting for the next day's cap.
6. If the chosen provider can't take mail, applies the stream's failover policy. Mail only moves to a fallback that
   passes its checks: available, terms that allow the stream's mail, a verified sending domain, enough quota and out of
   the sandbox. A fallback that is still warming up only takes mail when the policy accepts it.

The [mailbox provider](/reference/glossary/#mailbox-provider) (Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, iCloud or Other) comes from the
address's domain. Consumer domains such as `gmail.com` are known by name; a company's own domain is classified from its
MX records: Google Workspace counts as Gmail, Microsoft 365 as Outlook, Yahoo-hosted domains as Yahoo and iCloud custom
domains as iCloud, and the hosting is kept as a detail. A background sweep looks up new contacts' domains, and a send
looks up what's still waiting before it builds its audience. An address whose domain hasn't been looked up yet counts
as Other.

## Set up the routes

<Steps>

1. Open <UiPath path="nav.providers > providers.tabRouting" />. There is one card per stream, with the route tester and
   the settings shared across providers beside them.

   <Screenshot
     id="providers-routing"
     alt="Routing and failover for Broadcasts: a sticky 90/10 weighted split between Amazon SES and Postal, which is warming up on day 9 of 21, two conditions (one sends to Amazon SES only, the other to a 70/30 split), and the route tester answering Amazon SES for an Outlook address step by step."
   />

2. For Automations, System and Transactional, pick the <UiPath path="providers.routing.primary" /> and, if you have a
   second provider, the <UiPath path="providers.routing.fallback" />. The fallback takes over when the primary can't
   take mail.

3. For Broadcasts, set the <UiPath path="providers.routing.weightedSplit" />. Each row is a provider with its share in
   percent; the shares must add up to 100% before you can save. <UiPath path="providers.routing.addToSplit" /> adds a
   connected provider; a provider set to 0% can leave the split. Drag a row, or use its up and down arrows from the
   keyboard, to reorder it: the order only changes how the bar reads, and nobody changes provider. While a provider in
   the split [warms up](/providers/warm-up/), the warm-up planner raises its share on schedule and the card lists the
   planned steps.

4. Add conditions where one kind of mail should always use certain providers:
   <UiPath path="providers.routing.addCondition" />. Broadcasts and Automations can match on the list, the sender domain
   or the recipient's mailbox provider; Transactional matches on the transactional category or the mailbox provider.
   System mail has no conditions. Under the condition's provider, choose <UiPath path="providers.routing.thenOne" />, or
   <UiPath path="providers.routing.thenSplit" /> with a share per provider (a sticky split too). What you type under
   <UiPath path="providers.routing.why" /> is shown next to the condition, for example "Outlook is strict with new IPs".

5. Set the Broadcasts <UiPath path="providers.routing.failoverPolicy" />. The card states the trigger the health check
   really uses: at least half of the calls to the provider fail over 3 minutes (5 calls or more), or 2 health checks in a
   row fail. Silent webhooks alone raise an alarm, not a failover. Pick the
   <UiPath path="providers.routing.fallback" />, then what happens when the trigger fires
   (<UiPath path="providers.routing.whenTriggerFires" />):
   - <UiPath path="providers.routing.pauseAndAsk" />: the remaining recipients are held and you are asked. Moving a bulk
     send to a cold provider can hurt its reputation.
   - <UiPath path="providers.routing.warmOnly" />: the send moves on its own once the fallback has finished its warm-up;
     until then, it behaves like pause and ask.
   - <UiPath path="providers.routing.automatic" />.

   Under the policy, the card lists the checks the fallback must pass before any failover, each with a mark: available,
   terms that allow Broadcasts, sending domain verified, quota or today's warm-up cap left, warm, and not in the
   sandbox. If any check fails, Broadcasts pause and ask instead.

6. Automations, System and Transactional mail fail over to their fallback automatically, because it must keep going
   out; each card names its trigger, and traffic returns to the primary after 10 healthy minutes. Before the fallback
   takes over, it passes the same checks. If a check fails, ReputeCast keeps retrying the primary and alerts you instead
   of switching. Without a fallback, the mail waits until the provider recovers.

7. For Transactional mail, <UiPath path="providers.routing.firstInLine" /> makes transactional messages go first on a
   provider that also sends a campaign, within the provider's rate.

8. Check your changes before saving. As soon as you change something, a bar stays at the bottom of the page under
   <UiPath path="providers.routing.unsavedTitle" />. It says what saving changes for each stream, for example "Broadcasts
   split 90/10 → 85/15 · about 1,600 Broadcast recipients move from SES to Postal; nobody else changes provider", and for
   the other streams the share of their addresses (from the last 30 days) that would move. Every changed stream card carries an Unsaved change
   badge.

9. Choose <UiPath path="providers.routing.saveRouting" />, or <UiPath path="providers.routing.discard" /> to go back to
   the saved routing. Only the streams you changed are saved. New sends use the routing right away; sends already in
   progress keep their route.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution">
Before it saves, ReputeCast checks that every Broadcasts provider's terms allow bulk marketing. If one doesn't, it shows
the change and its warning and offers a fix (create a Postmark Broadcast stream, use another provider or save without a
fallback); your other edits stay while you fix it. Keeping it anyway is recorded in the audit log, and
[Send Readiness](/campaigns/send-readiness/) blocks every broadcast until the route changes. Read
[Choose a sending provider](/providers/choose/#bulk-marketing-terms).
</Aside>

## Test a route

The <UiPath path="providers.routing.testerTitle" /> tests the saved routing and, while you have unsaved changes, says
what they would change.

- **<UiPath path="providers.routing.tabRecipient" />**: pick the stream, a list and a sender domain (or a transactional
  template) and type an address. It answers with the provider, the mailbox provider and the MX host it was found from,
  the DKIM selector, the cost of one message, each step of the decision (the condition that matched, or the bucket the
  address hashes to in a split of two) and what happens if the provider fails. With an unsaved split, it adds whether
  this address stays on its provider or moves.
- **<UiPath path="providers.routing.tabCampaign" />**: runs the audience of the next broadcast (the next scheduled one, or
  the draft edited last) through the routing on its send day. It shows the recipients per provider, the conditions that matched, the
  sunset contacts left out, and what the send would do: recipients outside a warming provider's engagement tiers or over
  its cap going through a warm provider, recipients held by the cap until the next day's cap or while the plan is
  paused, and where a provider that can't send now would put its share. With unsaved changes, it says how many
  recipients move. A large audience is estimated from its first recipients and scaled.

## Check that it worked

- The page header shows when routing was saved and by whom.
- <UiPath path="nav.providers > providers.tabProviders" /> shows the routing per stream as a bar with each provider's
  share, the fallback and the failover policy.
- The route tester gives the provider you expect for an address on each list and mailbox provider that has a condition,
  and its Campaign tab shows the split you planned.
- During a send, the [live monitor](/campaigns/live-monitor/) shows each provider's share of the campaign.

## Troubleshooting

- **"Weights add up to 95%. Make them 100% to save."**: change a share so the total is 100. The same rule applies to a
  condition's split.
- **"Broadcasts changed while you were editing"**: someone saved the routing, or the warm-up planner raised a share,
  since you opened the page. Nothing was saved and your edits are still there. Choose
  <UiPath path="providers.routing.conflictShowLatest" /> to start again from the latest routing, or
  <UiPath path="providers.routing.conflictKeepMine" />. A change to another stream never blocks your save. Through the
  [save-routing API operation](/api/operations/save-routing/), each stream is saved with the version it was read at, and a stale one is refused with `409` and the code
  `routing_changed`.
- **"Couldn't refresh the routing"**: a background refresh failed. Your edits stay on the page, and mail keeps going out
  on the saved routing.
- **"Broadcasts has no primary provider"**: every stream except Transactional needs a primary. Transactional can stay
  without a route while your product sends no mail.
- **A stream card says a provider "can't take mail right now"**: a provider the route uses is down, paused or its
  webhooks are silent. Its mail fails over or waits as the policy says; when it is the fallback, the stream has no
  working fallback until it's back. Read [Provider health and outages](/providers/health-and-outages/).
- **A warning that the fallback is warming up**: the warm-up guard blocks failover to it until the day the warning
  names. With the
  warmed-up-only policy, Broadcasts pause and ask meanwhile.
- **The tester says the email would wait**: the provider is down or its webhooks are silent, so its share waits for it
  or for the fallback.
- **The tester says the address is suppressed**: nothing is sent to it on any provider. Read
  [The suppression list](/audience/suppressions/).
- **A Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 address shows as Other**: its domain hasn't been looked up yet, or the server
  can't reach DNS. Read [System requirements](/self-hosting/requirements/).
