Routing decides which [sending provider](/reference/glossary/#sending-provider) delivers each email: per stream, by list,
sender domain or the recipient's mailbox provider, with weighted splits and a fallback. This page sets up routes and
email failover multiple providers can take part in, then checks the result with the
[route tester](/reference/glossary/#route-tester) before you save.

## Before you begin

- Connect at least one provider; failover and splits need two. Read [Connect a sending provider](/providers/connect/).
- You need `providers:manage` to change routing. With `providers:view` you can open the page and use the route tester.

## How a route decides

Each email belongs to one of four [streams](/reference/glossary/#stream): Broadcasts (campaigns), Automations, System
(double opt-in confirmations, preference links, team invites) and Transactional (mail from your product). For every
recipient, ReputeCast:

1. Takes the stream's rules.
2. Checks the stream's conditions in order. The first one that matches decides the provider.
3. If no condition matches, uses the stream's default route: the primary provider, or a
   [weighted split](/reference/glossary/#weighted-split). A split puts each address in one of 100 buckets from a hash of
   the address, so the same recipient always lands on the same provider.
4. Checks the shared suppression list. A suppressed address gets nothing, whichever provider it was routed to.
5. If the chosen provider can't take mail, applies the stream's failover policy.

The mailbox provider (Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, iCloud or Other) comes from the contact's mail server records when the
contact exists, and from the address's domain otherwise.

## Set up the routes

<Steps>

1. Open <UiPath path="nav.providers > providers.tabRouting" />. There is one card per stream.

   <Screenshot
     id="providers-routing"
     alt="Routing and failover for Broadcasts: Amazon SES as primary with a 90/10 weighted split to Postal, two conditions, the failover policy set to pause and ask, and the route tester answering Amazon SES for an Outlook address."
   />

2. For each stream, pick the <UiPath path="providers.routing.primary" /> and, if you have a second provider, the
   <UiPath path="providers.routing.fallback" />. The fallback takes over when the primary is down; it can't also be part
   of the split.

3. Adjust a <UiPath path="providers.routing.weightedSplit" /> if the stream has one. Splits are created when you connect a
   provider and give it the Weighted split role on the Streams step (Broadcasts only). Here you change each provider's
   share; the primary gets the rest. Set a share to 0 to take the provider out of the split. While a provider in the
   split [warms up](/providers/warm-up/), the warm-up planner raises its share on schedule and the card says when.

4. Add conditions where one kind of mail should always use one provider: <UiPath path="providers.routing.addCondition" />.
   Broadcasts and Automations can match on the list, the sender domain or the recipient's mailbox provider;
   Transactional matches on the transactional category or the mailbox provider. System mail has no conditions. Each
   condition sends to exactly one provider, and what you type under <UiPath path="providers.routing.why" /> is shown next
   to it, for example "Outlook and iCloud are strict with new IPs".

5. Choose the Broadcasts <UiPath path="providers.routing.failoverPolicy" />:
   - **Pause and ask** (the default): if the primary fails during a send, the remaining recipients are held and you are
     notified. Moving a bulk send to a cold provider can hurt its reputation.
   - <UiPath path="providers.routing.warmOnly" />: moves the send to the fallback automatically once the fallback has
     finished its warm-up; until then, it behaves like pause and ask.
   - <UiPath path="providers.routing.automatic" />.

   Automations, System and Transactional mail fail over to their fallback automatically, because it must keep going
   out. Without a fallback, it waits until the provider recovers.

6. For Transactional mail, <UiPath path="providers.routing.firstInLine" /> makes transactional messages go first on a
   provider that also sends a campaign, within the provider's rate.

7. Try a few addresses in the <UiPath path="providers.routing.testerTitle" />: pick the stream, a list, a sender domain
   (or a transactional template) and type a recipient. It answers with the provider, the mailbox provider, the DKIM
   selector, the cost of one message, each step of the decision and what happens if the provider fails. It tests your
   unsaved changes.

8. Choose <UiPath path="providers.routing.saveRouting" />. New sends use the routing right away; sends already in
   progress keep their route.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution">
Before it saves, ReputeCast checks that every Broadcasts provider's terms allow bulk marketing. If one doesn't, it offers
a fix (create a Postmark Broadcast stream, use another provider or save without a fallback). Keeping it anyway is
recorded in the audit log, and [Send Readiness](/campaigns/send-readiness/) blocks every broadcast until the route
changes. Read [Choose a sending provider](/providers/choose/#bulk-marketing-terms).
</Aside>

## Check that it worked

- The page header shows when routing was saved and by whom.
- <UiPath path="nav.providers > providers.tabProviders" /> shows the routing per stream as a bar with each provider's
  share, the fallback and the failover policy.
- The route tester gives the provider you expect for an address on each list and mailbox provider that has a condition.
- During a send, the [live monitor](/campaigns/live-monitor/) shows each provider's share of the campaign.

## Troubleshooting

- **"The Broadcasts split adds up to 95%; it must add up to 100%"**: change a share so the total is 100.
- **"A provider appears twice in the split"** or **"The fallback is already part of the split"**: pick another provider,
  or none, as the fallback.
- **"Broadcasts has no primary provider"**: every stream except Transactional needs a primary. Transactional can stay
  without a route while your product sends no mail.
- **A warning that the fallback is warming up**: moving a broadcast to a provider that is still warming up can hurt its
  reputation. With the warmed-up-only policy, Broadcasts don't fail over to it until its warm-up ends; with Automatic,
  they do. The warning doesn't stop the save.
- **The route tester says the email would wait**: the provider is down or its webhooks are silent, so its share waits
  for it or for the fallback. Read [Provider health and outages](/providers/health-and-outages/).
- **The tester says the address is suppressed**: nothing is sent to it on any provider. Read
  [The suppression list](/audience/suppressions/).
