ReputeCast sends self-hosted transactional email (password resets, sign-in links, receipts and other product
mail) from the same instance as your newsletters, without sharing their reputation or their queue. Your app calls
the HTTP API or the SMTP relay with a template ID and data; ReputeCast renders a published
[transactional template](/reference/glossary/#transactional-template), checks suppressions and hands the message
to the sending providers on the Transactional route. Everything lives under <UiPath path="nav.transactional" />.

## How a transactional message is sent

1. **Your app calls ReputeCast.** It sends one message, or a batch of up to 100, with a
   [send-only key](/reference/glossary/#send-only-key) through the API, or connects to the
   [SMTP relay](/transactional/smtp-relay/). The API answers `202` with the message ID. The request format, the
   `Idempotency-Key` header and code samples are in [send transactional email with the API](/developers/transactional-api/).
2. **ReputeCast renders the live version.** Only published versions send. Missing required variables fail the call
   with `missing_variables`, and nothing is sent. Mail that arrives by SMTP is already rendered and is logged as
   **Raw (SMTP)**.
3. **Suppressions and categories are checked.** Transactional mail never goes to an address that hard bounced,
   complained, hit a spam trap, is invalid, or was added to the [suppression list](/audience/suppressions/) by hand
   or by import. It ignores marketing-only entries: unsubscribes and role addresses such as `billing@`. A recipient
   who turned an Optional category off doesn't get that category.
4. **The Transactional route delivers it.** Transactional is the fourth [stream](/reference/glossary/#stream),
   next to Broadcasts, Automations and System, with its own [route](/providers/routing/): a primary sending provider
   and an optional fallback. Failover is automatic. When the same sending provider also carries a campaign,
   transactional messages go first within its sending rate. Routing conditions on the mailbox provider read it from the
   recipient's MX records, so a Google Workspace address counts as Gmail.
   Transactional mail never waits for a [warm-up](/providers/warm-up/): a warming provider sends it within today's cap,
   counting toward it like other mail. Over the cap, or while the plan is paused, a message goes through a warm
   provider of its route that can take it, or else through the warming provider anyway, and the warm-up page counts it
   as over the cap.
5. **Events come back.** Delivery, bounce, complaint and engagement events update the message's status in the
   [message log](/transactional/message-log/) and are sent to your
   [webhook endpoints](/developers/webhooks/) as `message.*` events.

## Categories

Every template belongs to a category, and the category decides whether a recipient can turn it off.

| Kind      | Behavior                                                                                                     |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Essential | Always sent: sign-in, password, billing and account emails. The footer has no unsubscribe link.               |
| Optional  | Recipients can turn it off in the preference center or with the unsubscribe link in the footer.              |

You manage categories in [transactional settings](/transactional/settings/).

## Message statuses

A message is **Queued**, **Sent**, **Delivered**, **Deferred**, **Bounced**, **Complained**, **Rejected**,
**Failed** or **Test**. Rejected means ReputeCast stopped it before sending, for example because the address is
suppressed or the recipient opted out of the category. Test means a test-mode key sent it; test-mode keys deliver
only to workspace members and the test addresses you allow on the key.

## Alerts instead of auto-pause

Campaigns [auto-pause](/campaigns/live-monitor/) when bounces or complaints rise. Transactional mail never pauses,
because a password reset must keep arriving. Instead, ReputeCast checks every minute over the last hour and alerts
you when a template's bounce rate, the number of failed messages or the median delivery time passes its limit.

## When to change them

- Keep rendered content longer (up to 90 days) when support staff look up what a customer received; keep it shorter
  or not at all when content holds personal data. Change it in [transactional settings](/transactional/settings/).
- Lower the alert limits for templates where every failure matters, such as sign-in links.
- Give the Transactional route a fallback on a different sending provider so that an outage at one never stops
  password resets.
