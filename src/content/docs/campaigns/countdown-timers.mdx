The [Countdown block](/reference/glossary/#countdown-block) puts an email countdown timer in a campaign or an automation email: an image that ReputeCast draws each time the email is opened, so every reader sees the days, hours, minutes and seconds left to your deadline at the moment they look. Available from the next release after 1.6.0.

## Before you begin

- Adding the block and changing its settings needs `campaigns:edit`. Without it the settings are read-only, and the inspector says that only people who can edit campaigns change the Countdown block.
- The block is in the [composer](/campaigns/composer/) of campaigns, [recurring digest](/campaigns/recurring-digests/) templates and [automation emails](/automations/overview/). [Transactional templates](/transactional/templates/) don't offer it.
- Readers' mail apps load the image from your ReputeCast server's public address, so the server must be reachable from the internet, as it is for open tracking.

## Add the countdown

<Steps>

1. Open the campaign in the composer and select <UiPath path="composer.chrome.modeDesign" />.

2. In <UiPath path="composer.canvas.tabBlocks" />, under <UiPath path="composer.canvas.groupContent" />, drag <UiPath path="countdown.block" /> into a column, or select a column and press <kbd>Enter</kbd> on the tile. A new countdown ends a week from now at 23:59 in the workspace's time zone, and its image appears as soon as it is dropped.

3. With the block selected, set its <UiPath path="composer.tabStyle" />:

   - <UiPath path="countdown.endsLabel" />: the day and the time it ends, and the <UiPath path="countdown.zone" /> they are in (the workspace's is the default). Every reader counts down to the same moment wherever they are; the panel shows that moment in two other cities to check it.
   - <UiPath path="countdown.show" />: <UiPath path="countdown.dhms" /> (the default), <UiPath path="countdown.hms" /> or <UiPath path="countdown.dhm" />. Without days, the hours count whole days too, such as 52 hours.
   - <UiPath path="countdown.size" />: S (360 × 96 px), M (480 × 120 px) or L (600 × 150 px, as wide as the email). Phones scale M and L down.
   - <UiPath path="countdown.colours" />: the <UiPath path="countdown.background" />, the <UiPath path="countdown.digits" /> and the <UiPath path="countdown.labels" />, typed as `#RRGGBB` or picked. Under <UiPath path="countdown.brandKit" />, the button, text and background colors of your [brand kit](/campaigns/brand-kit/) apply to the color you last focused. Two chips show the contrast of the digits and of the labels on the background, such as "17.8:1 · Passes AA".
   - <UiPath path="countdown.whenEnds" />: <UiPath path="countdown.showZeros" />, or <UiPath path="countdown.showMessage" /> and type it in <UiPath path="countdown.message" />, up to 60 characters, such as "The sale has ended".
   - <UiPath path="countdown.alt" />: written for you from the end and the time zone, such as "Offer ends Sun 29 Nov at 23:59 (EST)". Type your own to replace it; clear the field to go back to the automatic one.

4. Check the line at the bottom of the panel, such as "A 600 × 150 GIF drawn when the email opens, the same for every reader". While the new image is being made it reads "Drawing the timer…".

5. Send yourself a [test](/campaigns/composer/#send-a-test) and open it: the timer counts down from the time left.

</Steps>

Each change asks ReputeCast for the image of the new settings half a second after your last edit, and the canvas shows it. The digits need a contrast of at least 4.5:1 on the background: a pair that falls short isn't drawn, and the panel says the image keeps the last digit color that passed until you pick a lighter color or a darker background. The labels have no minimum, but their chip says when they fail AA.

On the canvas the block's tag reads "Countdown" with its end, such as "Countdown Sun 29 Nov, 23:59 EST", and <UiPath path="composer.canvas.tabLayers" /> names it `mj-image · Countdown`. In <UiPath path="composer.tabSettings" /> it has a link, like any image; its picture and alt text come from its settings. Write mode shows it as a card with <UiPath path="composer.write.editInDesign" />.

## What readers see

The block is an image at `/t/cd/<token>.gif` on your server's public address, where the token is signed and names your workspace and the countdown. When a mail app loads it, ReputeCast draws an animated GIF from the time left at that second:

- up to 60 frames, one second apart, so the timer counts down for a minute while the email is open; the GIF plays once and stays on its last frame. Opening the email again shows the new time.
- the digits in IBM Plex Mono and the labels in IBM Plex Sans, in your colors, at the size you chose.
- once the countdown has ended, one frame with zeros or your message. Mail apps may keep that image for up to an hour; while it is running, the image is never cached.

The same settings always give the same image address, so a countdown copied into another email, or duplicated with its campaign, is the same timer. The first image of a set of settings is recorded in the [audit log](/team/audit-log/) as `countdown.created`. Scripts get the image of a set of settings with the [create countdown API operation](/api/operations/create-countdown/), which needs `campaigns:edit`. A mail app that doesn't animate GIFs shows the first frame, the time left when the email was opened; one that blocks images shows the alt text.

### Labels in each language

The labels under the digits (Days, Hours, Minutes, Seconds) and the automatic alt text follow the [language version](/campaigns/languages/) you are editing; the panel says which, for example "Labels follow the language you're editing: English." Open the German version and the block is drawn again with German labels. The labels come in English, German, Spanish, French and Vietnamese; a version in another language gets English labels. A message shown once the countdown ends uses the letters, digits and punctuation of Latin alphabets: a character the image's font can't draw is refused with "The countdown can't draw …".

## What the checks say

The composer, [Send Readiness](/reference/glossary/#send-readiness) and a flow's publish dialog check each countdown against the clock:

<TableScroll label="What each check says about a countdown">

| Where                                                     | Result | When                                                                                     | Says                                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The composer's <UiPath path="composer.chrome.tabChecks" /> | Warn   | The countdown has already ended.                                                         | "This countdown has already ended", with when it ended and what readers see instead. |
| [Send Readiness](/campaigns/send-readiness/), Content      | Block  | The countdown ends before the send starts: now, or the scheduled time.                   | "The countdown ends before the send starts"                                          |
| Send Readiness, Content                                    | Warn   | It ends within an hour of the send's start. Readers who open the email later see its end. | "The countdown ends within an hour of the send's start", with an acknowledgement      |
| Publishing an [automation](/automations/overview/)         | Warn   | One of the flow's countdowns ends within 30 days: the flow keeps sending it after that.  | "This flow keeps sending after …; the countdown will then show zeros"                 |

</TableScroll>

Both Send Readiness rows offer <UiPath path="countdown.ready.open" />, which opens the email in the composer. A Block can't be dismissed: change when the countdown ends, or remove the block.

<Aside type="note">
  A countdown ends at one moment for every email that carries it. Once that moment passes, every contact who reaches an
  automation email with it gets zeros or your message: change the email, or remove the block, before the deadline.
</Aside>

## Check that it worked

- The block shows the time left on the canvas, and the inspector ends with "A 600 × 150 GIF drawn when the email opens, the same for every reader" (or the size you chose).
- A test email opened a minute later shows a minute less.
- Send Readiness shows no countdown row, or a warning you have read and acknowledged.

## Troubleshooting

- **There is no Countdown tile.** You are editing a transactional template, which doesn't offer it, or the composer is in Write or Code mode. Switch to <UiPath path="composer.chrome.modeDesign" />.
- **"Digits need 4.5:1 against the background, so the image keeps …".** The digits are too close to the background. Pick a lighter digit color or a darker background; the chip turns to "Passes AA" at 4.5:1.
- **"The timer's image wasn't made: …".** ReputeCast didn't answer the change, for example after a dropped connection. Change the setting again, or reload the composer.
- **"Write what the countdown shows when it ends, in 60 characters or fewer."** The message is empty or too long.
- **The test shows a broken image.** The mail app couldn't reach your ReputeCast address. Check that the server's public address is reachable from the internet, as tracking links need.
- **Readers see zeros right away.** The countdown ended before they opened the email, or its time zone is wrong: check <UiPath path="countdown.zone" /> and the moment the panel shows for the other cities.
- **The labels are in English in a German email.** The block was drawn in another version. Open the German version in the composer; the block is drawn again in German.
