A newsletter signup form embed is two lines of HTML that render a ReputeCast form on your own website. You build the
form once, choose whether it sits in the page, opens as a popup or lives on a hosted page, and ReputeCast records the
exact [consent](/reference/glossary/#consent) text each person agreed to.

## Before you begin

- You need the `forms:edit` permission to create, change and publish forms. Owners, Admins and Editors have it.
- Create the lists people can join first. See [lists, consent and double opt-in](/audience/lists-and-consent/).
- Route the System stream to a sending provider, so [double opt-in](/reference/glossary/#double-opt-in)
  confirmations can go out. See [route mail across providers](/providers/routing/).
- Set your logo, colors and font in [branding](/forms/branding-and-domain/): every form uses them.

## Create and publish a form

Your workspace's hosted subscribe page works before you create any form. A form of your own lets you choose its
fields, lists and type.

<Steps>

1. Open <UiPath path="nav.forms > forms.tabs.signupForms" /> and select <UiPath path="forms.builder.newForm" />.

2. Name the form, choose its type and select <UiPath path="forms.builder.createForm" />:

   - An <UiPath path="forms.builder.kindEmbedded" /> form renders inside a page of your site.
   - A <UiPath path="forms.builder.kindPopup" /> form opens over your site after a delay, a scroll depth or when the
     visitor is about to leave; see [choose when a popup opens](#choose-when-a-popup-opens).
   - <UiPath path="forms.builder.kindHosted" /> lives on its own page at `/subscribe/<form-slug>` (the slug comes from
     the form's name), so you can link to it from anywhere without code.

   A new form starts with the email and first name fields, your first public list, double opt-in and the honeypot
   and timing check. It stays a draft until you publish it.

3. Under <UiPath path="forms.builder.fields" />, choose what the form asks for. Email is always required. Tick First
   name or Company, or use <UiPath path="forms.builder.addCustomField" /> for Last name, your workspace's custom fields
   and choice questions that tag contacts. A form holds up to 20 fields. To ask for them a step at a time, see
   [split a signup form into steps](/forms/form-steps/).

4. Under <UiPath path="forms.builder.lists" />, tick each list the form offers. Turn on
   <UiPath path="forms.builder.pickLists" /> to show a checkbox per list; when it is off, a signup joins every offered
   list. To choose which lists start checked, tick their boxes in the live preview.

5. Under <UiPath path="forms.builder.consent" />, edit the <UiPath path="forms.builder.consentText" />. The words
   "privacy policy" link to the privacy address from your branding. Every change starts a new consent version, and
   earlier signups keep the text they agreed to. ReputeCast stores the IP address, the time and the exact text as
   consent evidence.

6. Keep <UiPath path="forms.builder.doubleOptIn" /> on (the default) unless the people who sign up are already known
   to you. With it on, contacts stay pending until they confirm from the confirmation email (see
   [double opt-in confirmation](#double-opt-in-confirmation)). A contact who unsubscribed before must confirm even on
   a form without double opt-in.

7. Choose the checks under <UiPath path="forms.builder.spam" /> (see [spam protection](#spam-protection)).

8. Under <UiPath path="forms.builder.afterSubmit" />, show the built-in message ("check your inbox" with double
   opt-in, "you're subscribed" without it) or redirect to an address of your own, such as
   `https://acme.io/thanks`.

9. Under <UiPath path="forms.builder.allowedDomains" />, select <UiPath path="forms.builder.addDomain" /> for each
   site the form is embedded on, for example `acme.io`. Enter the host only. Subdomains need their own entry.

10. Select <UiPath path="forms.builder.savePublish" />.

</Steps>

## Choose when a popup opens

A popup opens on the first of its triggers:

- <UiPath path="forms.builder.opensAfter" />: seconds after the page loads, 8 by default.
- <UiPath path="forms.builder.orScrolled" />: how far down the page the visitor scrolled; 0 turns it off.
- <UiPath path="forms.builder.exitIntent" />: the pointer leaves the window through its top edge, as it does on the
  way to the tabs or the address bar. It works in desktop browsers only, with a mouse or a trackpad, and is armed 2
  seconds after the popup is ready, so a pointer that crosses the top while the page loads doesn't count. Phones and
  tablets have no exit intent: keep a delay or a scroll depth to show the popup there too.

With every trigger off, the popup opens after 8 seconds. After someone closes it, it stays closed in that browser for
the days in <UiPath path="forms.builder.closedFor" /> (14 by default), and it never opens again in a browser that
signed up through it.

### Hide the form from people who already signed up

<UiPath path="forms.builder.hideSubscribed" /> keeps a form away from a browser that signed up through any of your
workspace's forms; the browser remembers the signup in its local storage. A hidden form shows nothing and counts no
view. A new popup starts with it on, and a new embedded form or hosted page with it off, so you still see your own
embedded form after you test it. The setting works wherever the embed script shows the form; the hosted page itself
always shows its form.

## Show the form in steps

A form can ask for its fields in two to four steps, one step at a time, and ask choice questions whose answers add
tags to the contact. See [split a signup form into steps](/forms/form-steps/). Available from the next release after
1.6.0.

## Show the form only on some pages

Under <UiPath path="forms.builder.whereWhen" />, a popup or embedded form can show only on some pages, to visitors from
some campaigns or kinds of site, on some devices, to new or returning visitors, in some countries, to a segment, and
between two dates; a hosted page takes the dates. See
[show a signup form on chosen pages and dates](/forms/form-targeting/). Available from the next release after 1.6.0.

## Test versions of the form

<UiPath path="forms.builder.addVariant" /> starts an A/B test: up to three variants of the form's copy, steps and popup
timing, each browser always seeing the same one, and a result that names a winner only at 95% confidence by default.
See [A/B test a signup form](/forms/form-ab-tests/). Available from the next release after 1.6.0.

## Embed the form on your site

Under <UiPath path="forms.builder.embedCode" />, select <UiPath path="forms.builder.copyScript" /> and paste the code
into your site where the form should appear:

```html title="Embed code"
<div data-reputecast-form="frm_8c2e1a"></div>
<script async
  src="https://news.acme.io/p/acme-newsletter/forms/frm_8c2e1a.js">
</script>
```

The script is served from `/p/<workspace>/forms/<form id>.js` on your custom domain, or on your ReputeCast server's
public URL when you have none. Since 1.5.0, a custom domain carries only its own workspace's forms and pages, and a
domain another workspace on the server already uses can't become yours; see
[use your own domain](/forms/branding-and-domain/).

The script renders the form inside the `<div>` with your site's fonts. It loads no third-party code, uses no iframe
and sets no cookies. In the browser's local storage it keeps whether the browser signed up (through this form and
through any of your forms), when someone closed a popup, the day it first saw one of your forms (for new and returning
visitors) and a random id that A/B tests use. In the tab's session storage it keeps where the visit came from, for
[where and when](/forms/form-targeting/) rules.

- **Popups:** paste only the `<script>` element. The script opens a popup only when the page has no
  `data-reputecast-form` element; with the `<div>` in place, the form renders inline instead.
- **Language:** the form follows the `lang` attribute nearest to the `<div>` (or a `data-reputecast-lang` attribute
  on it). When the form isn't live in that language, it shows its fallback language.
- **Updates:** browsers can cache the script for 5 minutes, so a published change can take that long to reach your
  site.

### Use the plain HTML form

For a site that can't load scripts, select <UiPath path="forms.builder.copyHtml" />. You get the form as plain HTML in
one language. It posts to the form's hosted page, which shows the result:

```html title="Plain HTML form"
<form action="https://news.acme.io/p/acme-newsletter/subscribe/newsletter-signup" method="post" accept-charset="utf-8" lang="en">
  <input type="hidden" name="_plain" value="1">
  <input type="hidden" name="_lang" value="en">
  <label>Email <input type="email" name="email" autocomplete="email" required></label>
  <label>First name <input type="text" name="first_name" autocomplete="given-name"></label>
  <fieldset>
    <legend>What would you like?</legend>
    <label><input type="checkbox" name="list" value="01a1637b-5a5f-75a9-9c7b-b2786b3c5968" checked> Weekly Digest</label>
    <label><input type="checkbox" name="list" value="01a1637b-5a60-7ea9-9b79-848e29771b81"> Product Updates</label>
  </fieldset>
  <label><input type="checkbox" name="consent" value="1" required> I agree to receive emails from Acme. Unsubscribe any time with one click. See the <a href="https://acme.io/privacy">privacy policy</a>.</label>
  <div style="position:absolute;left:-10000px" aria-hidden="true"><input type="text" name="website" tabindex="-1" autocomplete="off"></div>
  <button type="submit">Subscribe</button>
</form>
```

Copy the HTML again after you change the form's fields, steps, lists or consent text: the copy on your site doesn't
update by itself. A form in several steps shows every step at once there, each as a fieldset.

A plain form can't carry the timing token, and any script could claim to be one, so since 1.5.0 its posts always count
as looking automated:

- When the workspace has Turnstile keys, every plain post goes through the Turnstile check, whatever the form's own
  spam settings.
- Without keys, plain posts have a budget of their own: 3 from one address, then one more every 20 minutes, and 30 to
  one form, then one more every 2 minutes. Past it, the hosted page shows the form filled in, to send again from there.
- A plain post always confirms by email, even on a form without double opt-in, and never signs anyone up for texts: the
  plain form has no SMS consent box.

### Link to the hosted page

Every form has a hosted page under <UiPath path="forms.builder.hostedPage" />, at
`/p/<workspace>/subscribe/<form-slug>`, or `/subscribe/<form-slug>` on your custom domain. The form chosen for your
workspace's own subscribe page answers at `/subscribe`. To change which form that is, see
[subscribe, preference and unsubscribe pages](/forms/public-pages/).

## Catch mistyped addresses

Since 1.6.0, when an address's domain is one typing mistake away from a large mailbox provider's (`gmail.com`,
`googlemail.com`, `outlook.com`, `hotmail.com`, `hotmail.co.uk`, `yahoo.com`, `yahoo.co.uk`, `icloud.com` or
`protonmail.com`), the form asks before it stores anything. Under the email field it says "Did you mean
linh@gmail.com?" with two buttons: "Use linh@gmail.com" signs up with the suggested address, "Keep linh@gmial.com" with
the address as typed. One mistake is a letter added, missing or wrong, or two neighbouring letters swapped. Real mail
domains one letter away too, such as `mail.com`, `gmx.com` or `web.de`, and disposable domains are never questioned.

The server makes the suggestion, so every way in asks the same question:

- The embedded form and the popup show the two buttons under the email field. Typing in the field again removes them.
- The hosted page shows them as two submit buttons, so it works without JavaScript. The page keeps the form's timing
  token, so a visitor who answers at once still passes the timing check.
- A post of the plain HTML form opens the hosted page with the question.

## Spam protection

<UiPath path="forms.builder.spam" /> has three checks:

- **Honeypot field and timing check** (on by default). A hidden field catches bots that fill in every input, and a
  submission sent less than 3 seconds after the form loaded looks automated. Bots that fill the honeypot see a
  success message, and nothing is saved.
- **Cloudflare Turnstile** (off by default). ReputeCast shows the Turnstile check only for submissions that already
  look automated, and holds them for 30 minutes while the person completes it. The keys are shared by every form of
  the workspace: paste the site key and secret key from the Cloudflare dashboard and select
  <UiPath path="forms.builder.saveKeys" />. The secret is stored encrypted and never shown again. When Cloudflare
  refuses the keys, the builder says so, and forms fall back to the honeypot and timing check until you paste working
  keys. When visitors' browsers report a Turnstile error from the check page, the builder shows how often and when, and
  Turnstile stays on: since 1.5.0, a browser's report can't switch it off. A report has ReputeCast ask Cloudflare, at
  most every 15 minutes, whether the stored secret still works, and only Cloudflare refusing the secret turns
  Turnstile off. Saving the keys again, or Cloudflare accepting a visitor's answer, clears the report.
- **Reject disposable domains and role addresses** (on by default). Throwaway inboxes and addresses such as `info@`
  are refused with a message that explains why.

Rate limits apply to every form: 5 submissions a minute from one IP address across the workspace's forms, and 120 a
minute per form. An IPv6 address counts by its /64 network, so a bot rotating addresses inside it stays one sender.
When a form passes its per-form limit, every submission must pass the Turnstile check until the flood stops, even on
forms that don't ask for it, as long as the workspace has Turnstile keys; without keys, the extra submissions are
refused. To check each address with a verification service as people sign up, see
[verify email addresses](/audience/verification/).

## Double opt-in confirmation

The confirmation email's <UiPath path="pagelabels.email.button" /> button opens a page that names the address and the
lists and asks once more. Only its <UiPath path="pagelabels.confirm.ask.button" /> button confirms. Mail security
scanners open every link in an email, so a link that confirmed on opening would confirm addresses that bots typed in.

- A link from a form confirms only the lists of that signup; lists picked on another form wait for that form's link.
- A link expires. An expired one opens "This link has expired" with
  <UiPath path="pagelabels.confirm.expired.button" />, which emails a fresh link if the address is still waiting.
- Each address gets a limited number of confirmation emails, whatever sends them: forms, reminders, the team's opt-in
  emails, imports and partner signups. The form still says "check your inbox". A second signup to the same lists
  within a day needs no new email, because the first link covers it; any other email held back goes out when the
  limit allows.
- The confirmation email uses your [branding](/forms/branding-and-domain/), the contact's language and the names of
  the lists still pending. The opt-in emails your team sends from the app (<UiPath path="audience.contacts.addContact" />,
  <UiPath path="audience.contacts.resendOptin" />, also for many contacts at once, or an import's re-permission) are the
  same email.
- Signups nobody confirms are erased after a while; see [set data retention](/team/workspaces/#set-data-retention).

How long a link works and how many emails an address gets are in the table below.

### Open the reader's inbox

Since 1.6.0, the "Check your inbox" message after a double opt-in signup offers the reader's web mail as its first
button, such as "Open Gmail", which opens in a new tab:

- Gmail opens a search for mail from the sender of your confirmation emails in the last hour.
- Yahoo Mail and Proton Mail open a search for mail from that sender.
- Outlook and iCloud Mail open the inbox.

Addresses at other domains, company domains and AOL addresses included, get no button. The server builds each link
from fixed addresses. A submission caught by the honeypot gets the same message with the same button, so a bot can't
tell it was caught. The embedded form, the popup and the hosted page show the button; a plain HTML form's post lands on
the hosted page's message.

Confirmation emails go out on the System stream. When no sending provider can send System mail yet, signups wait up to
3 days. The builder then lists the people still waiting, and <UiPath path="forms.builder.copyLinks" /> lets someone
with the `subscribers:edit` permission send each person their link by hand. Every copied link is logged.

## Check that it worked

1. Open a page of your site with the form and subscribe with an address you can read. With a Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo
   Mail, iCloud Mail or Proton Mail address, "Check your inbox" offers to open it.
2. Open the confirmation email, select its button, then <UiPath path="pagelabels.confirm.ask.button" />. The page
   that says you're subscribed opens.
3. In ReputeCast, find the address in the audience: the contact is on the form's lists, and its consent history shows
   the form, the consent version and the text. See [manage contacts](/audience/contacts/).
4. In the builder, the last 30 days show the view, the submission and the confirmed opt-in.

## Troubleshooting

- **The form says "This form can't be used on this site."** The site isn't in the allowed domains. Add its host under
  <UiPath path="forms.builder.allowedDomains" /> and save and publish. The builder also shows an alert with the
  number of rejected submissions per site, with buttons to allow the site or
  <UiPath path="forms.builder.keepBlocked" />.
- **The script answers 404.** The form is still a draft. Publish it.
- **A popup renders inside the page.** The page contains the `<div data-reputecast-form>` element. Remove it and keep
  only the `<script>` element.
- **A popup no longer opens for you.** After you close it, it stays closed for the days in
  <UiPath path="forms.builder.closedFor" /> (14 by default). It never opens again in a browser that signed up through
  it, nor, with <UiPath path="forms.builder.hideSubscribed" /> on, in one that signed up through any of your forms. Try
  a private window.
- **A popup never opens on phones.** Its only trigger is exit intent, which phones and tablets don't have. Add a delay
  or a scroll depth.
- **The form asks "Did you mean …?" about a real address.** Its domain is one letter away from a large mailbox
  provider's. The reader keeps the address as typed with the second button, and the signup goes through.
- **Visitors of the plain HTML form get the Turnstile check.** Since 1.5.0, every plain post does when the workspace has
  Turnstile keys. Use the embed script where the site can load it.
- **The builder says visitors' browsers reported a Turnstile error.** Check the site key and its hostnames in the
  Cloudflare dashboard, or paste another key. Turnstile stays on in the meantime.
- **Visitors see "This form isn't taking signups right now."** Someone selected
  <UiPath path="forms.builder.pauseSignups" />. Resume signups from the same menu.
- **Confirmation emails never arrive.** No sending provider is routed for the System stream. Connect one and route
  System mail to it: confirmations that have waited less than 3 days then go out. For older signups, use
  <UiPath path="forms.builder.copyLinks" />.
- **A reader signed up twice but got one confirmation email.** Each address gets a limited number of confirmation
  emails (see [defaults and limits](#defaults-and-limits)). The first email's link still works.
- **A reader's confirmation link says it has expired.** They select
  <UiPath path="pagelabels.confirm.expired.button" /> on that page, and a fresh link reaches the address if it is still
  waiting to confirm.
