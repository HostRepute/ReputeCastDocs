An email holdout group is a random share of your [contacts](/reference/glossary/#contact) kept out of your campaigns,
your automation emails or both, for as long as it runs. Comparing what they bought with what everyone else bought
shows what your marketing adds: <UiPath path="nav.audience > holdout.tab" /> measures it every night and says, for
example, "Marketing added 18% more revenue per contact (96% confidence)." Available from the next release after 1.6.0.

## How it works

1. **A bucket per contact.** ReputeCast puts each contact in one of 10,000 buckets, worked out from the contact's id
   and the group's random seed. While the holdout group is on, the contacts in the buckets under its share are held
   out: 5% means buckets 0 to 499. New contacts fall into a bucket the same way, so the share holds as your audience
   grows.
2. **One rule everywhere.** The sending engine, [Send Readiness](/reference/glossary/#send-readiness), automations,
   text, push and WhatsApp campaigns, the segment field and the measurement all ask the same database function, so
   they never disagree about who is held out.
3. **Periods.** Each set of settings is one period. Changing the share or the scope, or turning the group off, ends the
   period, and the next one starts with the same seed: raising the share keeps everyone already held out and adds more;
   lowering it lets some of them go. Turning the group on again later holds out the same people. <UiPath path="sendingRules.holdout.dialogConfirm" /> draws
   a new seed, so a new random set of people is held out from then on.
4. **Sends leave the group out.** What each kind of send does is below. Transactional and System mail always reach
   everyone.
5. **The measurement.** Every night at 04:00 in the workspace's time zone, ReputeCast counts what each group did in
   the open period and the last closed one. The results page compares the holdout group with everyone else, the
   _marketing group_, and tests whether the difference is more than chance.

### What each send does

<TableScroll label="How each kind of send treats the holdout group">

| Send | With the holdout group on |
| --- | --- |
| Email campaigns | Held-out contacts are left out right after duplicates, at the same step in Send Readiness's count and the sending engine. The share is taken when the send starts building its audience. <UiPath path="composer.reviewSend" /> shows the line "Holdout group: 617 left out (5%)". |
| An email campaign with <UiPath path="review.includeHoldout" /> | Goes to the holdout group too. The choice is part of the campaign's audience, so [approvals](/campaigns/approvals/) compare it and the confirmation names it. It can't change while the campaign is scheduled. |
| Text, push and WhatsApp campaigns | Messages to held-out contacts are logged as skipped, with the reason holdout. |
| Automation email, text, push and WhatsApp steps | A held-out contact passes the step without a message and goes on through the flow. The step counts them: "67 held out" on the canvas. |
| Transactional and System mail | Always sent. |

</TableScroll>

Use <UiPath path="review.includeHoldout" /> only for messages everyone must get, such as a change to your terms. A
campaign that included the group adds nothing to the difference, and the results page counts these campaigns. See
[schedule and send a campaign](/campaigns/schedule-and-send/).

## Set up the holdout group

The holdout group is set in <UiPath path="nav.settings > settings.sendingRules > sendingRules.holdout.title" />, which
needs `workspace:manage` (Owner and Admin). [Add workspace sending rules](/campaigns/sending-rules/) describes the card
in full.

<Steps>

1. Turn on the <UiPath path="sendingRules.holdout.title" /> switch. It saves at once, with 5% of contacts held out of
   both campaigns and automations the first time.

2. Type the share, from 0.1% to 20% with one decimal, and under <UiPath path="sendingRules.holdout.scope" /> tick
   <UiPath path="sendingRules.holdout.campaigns" />, <UiPath path="sendingRules.holdout.automations" /> or both.
   Select <UiPath path="sendingRules.holdout.save" />. A new period starts.

3. To hold out other people, select <UiPath path="sendingRules.holdout.startOver" />, optionally type why (kept in the
   audit log), and select <UiPath path="sendingRules.holdout.dialogConfirm" />. The current group ends today and its
   results are kept; the measurement starts again from today.

</Steps>

The card then says how many contacts are held out and since when. Every change is in the audit log. Campaigns pick a
change up from their next audience count, automations from their next step.

### How the numbers are counted

- **Who is counted.** Every contact that wasn't erased, existed before the window ended and wasn't on the
  [suppression list](/audience/suppressions/) before it started, in the holdout group or the marketing group by their
  bucket.
- **A converter** placed a store order (not a test, not cancelled), recorded a
  [conversion](/developers/events-and-conversions/) or started a paid subscription through Stripe in the window.
- **Revenue** is orders minus refunds plus conversion values, in the main currency set in Earn's settings (US dollars
  until one is set).
- **A site visitor** was seen on one of the workspace's tracked sites in the window.
- **The tests.** Conversion rate and site visitors use a two-proportion z-test; revenue and orders per contact use
  Welch's t-test, which doesn't assume both groups vary alike. Confidence is 1 minus the two-sided p-value, and a
  difference counts at 95%.

### The verdict

<TableScroll label="Holdout verdicts">

| Verdict | When |
| --- | --- |
| "Marketing added 18% more revenue per contact (96% confidence)." | Revenue per contact is higher in the marketing group, with 95% confidence or more. |
| "Contacts in the holdout brought 8% more revenue per contact (95% confidence)." | Revenue per contact is higher in the holdout group, with 95% confidence or more. |
| "No measurable difference yet." | The difference could be chance. |
| "Too few contacts in the holdout: 400 of 1,000 needed." | The holdout group has fewer than 1,000 contacts. Raise the share, or wait for the audience to grow. |
| "Too early to tell: the holdout group started 1 Nov. Give it at least two weeks." | The open period is less than 14 days old. |
| "No results yet. ReputeCast computes them every night at 04:00, or now with Recompute." | The period hasn't been measured. |

</TableScroll>

## Recompute the results

Select <UiPath path="holdout.recompute" /> to measure now instead of waiting for 04:00. It runs in the background,
"Computing the results… This takes a minute." shows meanwhile, and the page loads the new results when they are ready.
You can recompute once every 10 minutes per workspace; sooner, the page says when the results were last computed.
The nightly run also catches up as soon as it can when the server was off at 04:00.

## Find held-out contacts

Segments have the field **In the holdout group**, _is Yes_ or _is No_, for the open period whatever it holds contacts
out of; SQL segments read it as the `in_holdout` column of `contacts`. Use it to see who is held out. See
[build segments](/audience/segments/).

## When to change them

- Keep the share at 5% or more for an audience of 20,000 contacts or fewer, so the holdout group reaches the 1,000
  contacts a verdict needs. A large audience can hold out less, such as 2%, and lose less revenue.
- Hold out of campaigns only to measure your newsletters, or of automations only to measure your flows. Measuring both
  together tells you what all your email adds.
- Leave a period running for at least two weeks, and longer for products people buy rarely: every change of share or
  scope starts the results again.
- Start over when the same people have been held out for months and you want fresh ones.
