A [DMARC aggregate report](/reference/glossary/#dmarc-aggregate-report) tells you which servers sent mail as your
domain and whether that mail passed DMARC. This guide shows how to collect the reports, bring them into ReputeCast
and read them under <UiPath path="nav.deliverability > placement.health" />, so you can move your
[DMARC](/reference/glossary/#dmarc) policy from `p=none` to `quarantine` or `reject` without losing real mail.

Mailbox providers that support DMARC send these reports as XML files, by default once a day, to the addresses in the
`rua` tag of your DMARC record ([RFC 7489, section 6.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7489#section-6.3), checked
October 2026). ReputeCast has no inbox of its own for them: either [HostRepute](/reference/glossary/#hostrepute)
collects and parses them for you, or you receive them in your own mailbox and upload them.

## Before you begin

- Your sending domain publishes a DMARC record. If it doesn't, start with
  [Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC](/deliverability/spf-dkim-dmarc/).
- Reading the reports needs `deliverability:view`, which every built-in role has. Uploading a report needs
  `domains:manage` (Owner and Admin).
- Decide where the reports go: HostRepute, a mailbox you own, or both. `rua` takes several addresses separated by
  commas.

## Collect the reports

### Through HostRepute

<Steps>

1. Connect HostRepute ([Connect HostRepute](/deliverability/hostrepute/)). On an empty DMARC card,
   <UiPath path="deliverability.dmarc.useHostRepute" /> takes you there.

2. Copy the report address HostRepute gives your account. The DMARC card shows it ("aggregate reports to …"), and
   ReputeCast's suggested DMARC record includes it as `rua=mailto:…` once HostRepute is connected.

3. Add the address to the `rua` tag of your DMARC record at your DNS host.

</Steps>

HostRepute parses the reports, and ReputeCast reads the last 7 days of results with its hourly sync.

### In your own mailbox

<Steps>

1. Add a mailbox you own to the `rua` tag:

   ```txt title="_dmarc.acme.io TXT"
   v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@acme.io
   ```

2. If the mailbox is on a different organizational domain than the DMARC record, that other domain has to accept
   the reports by publishing a TXT record `v=DMARC1` at `<your domain>._report._dmarc.<its domain>`, for example
   `acme.io._report._dmarc.example.net`. Without it, mailbox providers don't send the reports there
   ([RFC 7489, section 7.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7489#section-7.1), checked October 2026).

3. Save the report attachments as they arrive. They usually come as `.xml.gz` or `.zip` files.

</Steps>

## Upload a report

<Steps>

1. Open <UiPath path="nav.deliverability > placement.health" />. While no report covers the last 7 days, the card
   reads **DMARC report summary** with the badge **No reports yet**.

2. Select <UiPath path="deliverability.dmarc.uploadButton" /> and choose one file: the `.xml` report, or the `.xml.gz`
   or `.zip` it came in. A `.zip` must hold the `.xml` report; ReputeCast reads the first one inside. The file can be
   up to 20 MiB, compressed and uncompressed.

3. Select <UiPath path="deliverability.dmarc.upload" />. The notice names the organization that sent the report, the
   number of messages and the domain.

</Steps>

ReputeCast stores only the report's counts: the policy that was published, and per sending IP the message count,
what the receiver did, and the SPF and DKIM results. Uploading the same report again (same organization and report ID)
replaces the earlier copy, so nothing is counted twice.

The upload button exists only on the empty card. Once reports from the last 7 days are shown, upload further reports
with the API operation [Upload a DMARC aggregate report](/api/operations/upload-dmarc-report/), which takes the raw file and needs
an API key with `domains:manage`:

```sh title="Upload a DMARC aggregate report with the API"
curl -X POST "https://reputecast.example.com/api/v1/workspaces/acme-newsletter/deliverability/dmarc/reports" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $REPUTECAST_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/octet-stream" \
  --data-binary @report.xml.gz
```

A successful upload returns `201` with the organization, report ID, domain, period and message count. See
[Authentication and API keys](/developers/api-authentication/) to create a key.

The card is titled **DMARC reports · last 7 days** and summarizes every report whose period ended in the last 7 days.
Its badge is the pass rate: the share of messages that passed DMARC through SPF or DKIM. It is green from 98%, amber
from 90%, and red below.

<TableScroll label="DMARC card columns">

| Column   | What it shows                                                                                                                                   |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Source   | The sending provider for its shared pool (`Amazon SES`), the provider and IP for a dedicated IP (`Postal · 203.0.113.25`), or `Unknown · <IP>`. |
| Messages | Messages from that source in the reports.                                                                                                       |
| SPF      | `aligned` when at least half of the messages passed SPF in line with the From domain; otherwise the most common result, such as `fail`.       |
| DKIM     | The same, for DKIM.                                                                                                                             |
| Result   | `pass` when SPF or DKIM is aligned. Otherwise `quarantined` or `rejected` when at least half were treated that way, else `fail`.               |

</TableScroll>

Only mail with a From domain of this workspace counts. Known sending providers come first, by volume, then unknown
sources, which are tinted red and marked "not one of your providers".

<Aside type="caution" title="Shared pools in uploaded reports">
ReputeCast recognizes a shared pool, such as Amazon SES or Postmark, by the reverse DNS name of the sending IP.
Uploaded reports don't carry that name, so mail from shared pools shows up as `Unknown · <IP>` in uploaded reports.
Only IPs listed on a sending provider, such as your dedicated IPs, are recognized. Look an unknown IP up before you
treat it as a stranger.
</Aside>

### Investigate a source

When an unknown source sent mail as your domain, the card adds a line under the table, for example "Someone is sending
as news.acme.io from an unknown host; p=quarantine kept it out of inboxes." With `p=none` it says receivers may still
deliver that mail.

<Steps>

1. Select <UiPath path="deliverability.dmarc.investigate" />.

2. Read the dialog: messages and reports in the last 7 days, the dates seen, which organizations reported it, the
   reverse DNS name, the From domains used, the SPF and DKIM results with their domains, and what receivers did with
   the mail (delivered, quarantined or rejected).

3. Decide what the source is:
   - **A service you use**, such as a CRM, a help desk or an invoicing tool: add it to the SPF record of the domain
     and turn on DKIM signing in that service.
   - **Not yours:** nothing to fix on your side. If receivers still delivered the mail, raise your DMARC policy.

</Steps>

## Move to quarantine or reject

Raise the policy once every source you recognize shows `aligned` for SPF or DKIM, and only unknown sources fail.
Microsoft advises moving in steps, from `none` to `quarantine` to `reject`, to avoid losing mail
([Microsoft, "Outlook's new requirements for high-volume senders"](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%E2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%E2%80%90volume-senders/4399730),
FAQ, checked October 2026).

<Steps>

1. Fix the remaining sources you recognize, as in [Investigate a source](#investigate-a-source).

2. Change `p=none` to `p=quarantine` in the DMARC record at your DNS host. Keep the `rua` tag:

   ```txt title="_dmarc.acme.io TXT"
   v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@acme.io
   ```

3. Watch the card for a few weeks. Mail that fails now shows `quarantined` in the Result column.

4. When nothing legitimate is quarantined, change the policy to `p=reject`.

</Steps>

[BIMI](/deliverability/bimi/) needs `p=quarantine` or `p=reject` applied to all mail, so don't lower `pct` below 100
if you plan to show your logo.

## Check that it worked

- In the domains table under <UiPath path="nav.settings > settings.senders" />, the DMARC column shows the new policy.
  `p=none` is shown as a warning, `p=quarantine` and `p=reject` as passing.
- In [Send Readiness](/campaigns/send-readiness/), the DMARC row reads, for example, "DMARC p=quarantine, 99.6% of mail
  aligned over the last 7 days". Under 90% aligned, it warns.
- The domain card on the Health tab shows **DMARC alignment, 7 d** with the same percentage.

## Troubleshooting

- **No reports arrive.** Check the `rua` address for typos and, for a mailbox on another domain, the
  `_report._dmarc` record from [In your own mailbox](#in-your-own-mailbox). Reports are usually sent once a day, so
  wait at least a day after publishing.
- **"This report is for …, which isn't a sending domain of this workspace."** The report's domain doesn't share an
  organizational domain with any domain of this workspace. Upload it in the workspace that sends as that domain, or
  [add the domain](/providers/senders-and-domains/) first.
- **"This isn't a DMARC aggregate report."** Only aggregate (`rua`) reports are accepted. Failure reports from `ruf`
  and other XML files are refused.
- **"The zip file has no .xml report."** Extract the archive and upload the `.xml` file.
- **The upload button is missing or disabled.** The card already shows reports from the last 7 days (use the API), or
  you don't have `domains:manage`; the disabled button says who can help.
- **Amazon SES or Postmark shows as Unknown.** The report was uploaded; see
  [Shared pools in uploaded reports](#read-the-dmarc-card).
