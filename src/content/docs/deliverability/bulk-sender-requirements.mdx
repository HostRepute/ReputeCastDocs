Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft bulk sender requirements set the minimum for mail to reach their users: authenticated,
aligned with the From address, with a working unsubscribe, and rarely reported as spam. This page lists the rules of
each [mailbox provider](/reference/glossary/#mailbox-provider) and shows where
[Send Readiness](/reference/glossary/#send-readiness) checks each one before a campaign goes out. The rules below were
checked in October 2026; mailbox providers change them, so follow the linked sources for the current text.

## Who counts as a bulk sender

- **Gmail:** anyone who sends close to 5,000 messages or more to personal Gmail accounts within 24 hours. Messages
  from all subdomains of the same primary domain count together, and the status is permanent once reached. The rules
  apply to personal Gmail accounts, not to Google Workspace accounts. Since November 2025, Gmail has been ramping up
  enforcement, including temporary and permanent rejections of mail that doesn't meet them
  ([Google, "Email sender guidelines"](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126) and
  [FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414)).
- **Yahoo:** Yahoo doesn't publish a number; a bulk sender sends "a significant volume of mail", judged by the
  authenticated domain or the From domain ([Yahoo Sender Hub, "Sender Best Practices"](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)
  and [FAQ](https://senders.yahooinc.com/faqs/)).
- **Microsoft:** domains that send more than 5,000 messages a day to Outlook.com consumer addresses (hotmail.com,
  live.com, outlook.com). Since May 5, 2025, mail that fails the authentication rules is rejected with
  `550; 5.7.515 Access denied, sending domain [SendingDomain] does not meet the required authentication level.`
  ([Microsoft, "Outlook's new requirements for high-volume senders"](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%E2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%E2%80%90volume-senders/4399730)).
  See [Outlook 550 5.7.515](/smtp-errors/outlook-550-5-7-515/).

ReputeCast doesn't count your daily volume against these thresholds. Send Readiness applies the same checks to every
email campaign, whatever its size, so the rules it covers are already in place when your list grows past 5,000.

### How the Send Readiness checks decide

- **Authentication** checks the stored DNS records of the campaign's sending domain against what each sending
  provider in the route needs. A missing SPF authorization, unverified DKIM, a missing or invalid DMARC record, or a
  provider whose mail can't align with DMARC is a Block: the send can't start. A DNS lookup that fails is Unknown, not a
  Block.
- **Compliance** has three rows. "One-click unsubscribe headers present" passes when the instance's public URL uses
  HTTPS and every sending provider of the route signs the headers with DKIM. The footer row looks for an unsubscribe
  link and a postal address in the compiled email of every version. The bulk-sender row passes as "Bulk-sender
  requirements met" when DMARC passes and the complaint rate is under 0.1%.
- **The complaint rate** in the bulk-sender row is the 7-day complaint rate of the route's sending providers, weighted
  by recipients, and counts only mail delivered to mailbox providers that report complaints. Gmail and iCloud never
  report them, so their mail is left out of the rate; with no reporting mail the row says the rate isn't measured yet.
- **Gmail's spam rate** comes from Google Postmaster Tools, a day late, when [HostRepute](/deliverability/hostrepute/)
  brings Postmaster data for the sending domain (or its parent). It warns and blocks at the same levels.
- **Gmail Postmaster's compliance status** shows on the bulk-sender row when Postmaster has data for the domain:
  "Gmail Postmaster: Compliant (yesterday)", or "Needs work", which warns, when Google measured SPF, DKIM, DMARC or TLS
  under 98% of mail or a spam rate of 0.3% or more. Yahoo has no such report, so the row also lists the same rules
  ReputeCast checks itself: DMARC aligned, one-click unsubscribe and the complaint rate. Without Postmaster data the row
  says Gmail Postmaster isn't connected, with <UiPath path="readiness.fix.connectPostmaster" />, or that it has no data for the domain yet; Google
  reports only domains with enough Gmail volume.

<Aside type="note" title="Rules that depend on your sending provider">
Reverse DNS and TLS belong to the servers that hand your mail to Gmail, Yahoo and Outlook.com. With a shared IP pool,
such as Amazon SES without dedicated IPs, the sending provider manages both. With dedicated IPs or your own Postal or
SMTP server, set up the PTR record of each IP with whoever hosts it and check the result on the Health page.
</Aside>

## Defaults and limits

## When to change them

You can't change the Send Readiness limits. You can tighten the auto-pause thresholds per workspace in
<UiPath path="nav.settings > settings.providers" /> or per campaign in <UiPath path="composer.reviewSend" />. Keep the
complaint threshold at 0.10% or lower: Gmail recommends staying under 0.1% and makes bulk senders above 0.3% ineligible
for its delivery mitigation. If Gmail starts deferring mail with a rate limit such as
[Gmail 421 4.7.28](/smtp-errors/gmail-421-4-7-28/), slow down with a [warm-up plan](/providers/warm-up/) instead of
raising thresholds.
