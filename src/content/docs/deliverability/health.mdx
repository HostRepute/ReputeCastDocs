<UiPath path="nav.deliverability > placement.health" /> is where ReputeCast does its domain reputation monitoring: one
page with the state of every [sending domain](/reference/glossary/#sending-domain) and
[dedicated IP](/reference/glossary/#dedicated-ip), the account health of each
[sending provider](/reference/glossary/#sending-provider), blocklists, DMARC reports and recent incidents. This guide
shows how to run the checks, read each section and export the results. Everything on the page works without
HostRepute; connecting it adds more sources and data.

## Before you begin

- You need `deliverability:view`. The Owner, Admin, Editor and Viewer roles have it, and so do client roles.
- Connect a [sending provider](/providers/connect/) and add a [sending domain](/providers/senders-and-domains/) first.
  Until then the page has nothing to show.
- Optional: [connect HostRepute](/deliverability/hostrepute/) for 84 blocklist sources checked every hour, Google
  Postmaster Tools, Microsoft SNDS and collected DMARC reports.

## Check your sending health

<Steps>

1. Open <UiPath path="nav.deliverability > placement.health" />. The badge next to the tabs tells you where the data
   comes from:

   - **HostRepute connected · hourly checks**, with the time of the last and the next full check.
   - **HostRepute not connected · provider data and built-in checks**, with the time of the last daily check.
   - **HostRepute needs reconnecting** or **HostRepute unreachable · cached results**: the page shows the last data
     HostRepute sent, marked as stale.

2. Select <UiPath path="deliverability.runChecks" /> to check now. With HostRepute connected, the button reads
   <UiPath path="deliverability.runAll" /> and asks HostRepute to check again as well. A progress line shows how many
   sources have answered, and a notice says when the checks finished.

3. Read the page from top to bottom, as described in [What each section shows](#what-each-section-shows). Cards with
   an amber or red border need attention first.

4. To keep the results, select <UiPath path="deliverability.exportReport" />. See
   [Export the results](#export-the-results).

</Steps>

## What each section shows

### Health per sending domain and IP

<UiPath path="deliverability.assetsTitle" /> has one card per sending domain and per dedicated IP. A domain that is
only the parent of other sending domains gets no card of its own.

- **Sending domain:** which sending providers use it, how many domain blocklists list it, the SPF, DKIM and DMARC
  results (for example `pass · pass · p=quarantine`), and the share of mail aligned with DMARC over 7 days. That share
  needs [DMARC aggregate reports](/deliverability/dmarc-reports/). With HostRepute connected, Gmail's domain
  reputation is added.
- **Dedicated IP:** the IP blocklists, the reverse DNS result (`matches` when the PTR name resolves back to the IP,
  `doesn't resolve back`, or `missing`) and, with HostRepute, Microsoft SNDS. A warming IP links to its
  <UiPath path="deliverability.warmupPlan" />.
- **Shared pool:** a sending provider's shared IPs, such as Amazon SES without dedicated IPs, get a card named after
  the pool, such as "Amazon SES pool", with the badge **Shared IPs**. They are checked at domain level, because the
  sending provider manages those IPs.

The badge of a card shows a score and a verdict (Good, Fair, Poor, Bad) when HostRepute supplies them. Without
HostRepute, a domain shows Good or Needs attention. The border turns amber for any listing, SPF or DKIM not passing, or
a missing or invalid DMARC record, and red for a listing on a high-impact list or stale HostRepute data. With HostRepute
connected, a domain it doesn't watch yet shows **Not monitored** and the button
<UiPath path="hostrepute.addToMonitoring" />.

### Account health per sending provider

<UiPath path="deliverability.providerHealthTitle" /> shows each sending provider's status (Healthy, Degraded, Down,
Sandbox, Warming up, Paused) and two rates over the last 7 days:

- **Bounce rate:** bounced messages divided by messages sent.
- **Complaint rate:** complaints divided by messages delivered to [mailbox providers](/reference/glossary/#mailbox-provider)
  that report complaints. Gmail and iCloud never do, so their mail is left out; a provider whose mail went only there, or
  that delivered nothing, shows no complaint rate instead of 0%.

When the sending provider reports its own rate and that figure is less than 48 hours old, ReputeCast shows it instead.
ReputeCast judges its own rates only once it has counted 500 messages in the 7 days: below that, the card shows the rates
without a bar and says how many messages there were. A bounce among three messages isn't a 33% bounce rate.
Each bar fills toward the provider's limit. For Amazon SES, ReputeCast sets the levels at which AWS reviews and pauses
an account: bounces 5% (review) and 10% (pause), complaints 0.1% and 0.5%. Other sending providers get a 5% bounce and
0.1% complaint limit when you connect them; you can change both in the provider's settings under
<UiPath path="nav.providers" />.

[Send Readiness](/reference/glossary/#send-readiness) reads the same rates by the same 500-message rule: it warns from
80% of a limit and blocks a send at the Amazon SES pause level. See [Provider health and outages](/providers/health-and-outages/) and
[Bounces, complaints and provider limits](/deliverability/bounces-and-complaints/).

### Blocklists

The blocklist table lists each source with what it checked, its status, since when an asset is listed (with
HostRepute), and the list's delisting page. Without HostRepute, ReputeCast queries 8 public lists once a day. See
[Check and fix blocklist listings](/deliverability/blocklists/).

### Google Postmaster Tools and Microsoft SNDS

These two cards are filled by HostRepute; without it, they show **No data yet** and
<UiPath path="deliverability.connectHostRepute" />.

- **Google Postmaster Tools** for the first sending domain: domain reputation, IP reputation of the pool, spam rate
  over 30 days with a chart, SPF, DKIM and DMARC success, TLS for incoming mail, and campaigns flagged by Gmail's
  feedback loop.
- **Microsoft SNDS** for a dedicated IP: the filter result (Green, Yellow, Red), messages and complaint rate over 7
  days, spam trap hits and JMRP feedback reports. Shared pools aren't covered by SNDS.

### DMARC reports

The DMARC card lists the sources that sent mail as your domains over the last 7 days, with their SPF, DKIM and DMARC
results. See [Read DMARC aggregate reports](/deliverability/dmarc-reports/).

### Incident timeline

<UiPath path="deliverability.incidentsTitle" /> lists the latest incidents of the last 45 days, each linked to the
campaign, report or sending provider it concerns:

- a new blocklist listing found by the built-in check, and HostRepute alerts (listings, delistings, reputation drops,
  DMARC failures, authentication changes);
- a campaign [auto-paused](/campaigns/live-monitor/), a Broadcast share held for a decision or moved to another
  sending provider;
- a sending provider going down, failing over, coming back, or sending no webhook events while mail went out.

## When checks run

- **Without HostRepute:** about once a day, ReputeCast checks the DNS authentication of each sending domain (SPF,
  DKIM, DMARC, BIMI, return path), the reverse DNS of dedicated IPs, and the 8 built-in blocklists.
- **With HostRepute:** HostRepute's results are synced every hour; ReputeCast still checks DNS authentication and
  reverse DNS itself about once a day. If a sync fails three times, the built-in blocklist check runs instead.
- **Domain records** under <UiPath path="nav.settings > settings.senders" /> are checked separately, every 30 minutes
  ([Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC](/deliverability/spf-dkim-dmarc/)).

## Export the results

<UiPath path="deliverability.exportReport" /> downloads `deliverability-<workspace>-<date>.csv` with the columns
`section`, `asset`, `check`, `status`, `detail`, `since` and `checked_at`. It has one row per check: SPF, DKIM and DMARC
per domain, reverse DNS per IP, DMARC alignment and sources, each blocklist, bounce and complaint rates per sending
provider, Postmaster and SNDS figures, and the incidents shown on the page.

## Check that it worked

- The notice says the checks finished, with the number of listings found or "clean on" the number of sources.
- The caption next to the badge shows the time of the new check.
- The CSV opens with one row per check.

## Troubleshooting

- **The buttons are disabled with "Connect a sending provider first".** No sending domain, dedicated IP or sending
  provider exists yet. [Connect a sending provider](/providers/connect/).
- **The run says some lookups didn't answer.** They show as unknown, not clean. The instance's DNS resolver is probably
  a public one that blocklists refuse; see [Check and fix blocklist listings](/deliverability/blocklists/).
- **The badge says HostRepute needs reconnecting or is unreachable.** Reconnect it, or select
  <UiPath path="deliverability.useBuiltin" />. Meanwhile Send Readiness marks blocklist checks as Unknown, never as a
  Block.
- **Postmaster and SNDS stay empty.** They need HostRepute and appear after its first sync. SNDS only covers dedicated
  IPs.
- **Reverse DNS says the PTR name doesn't resolve back.** The A or AAAA record of the PTR name doesn't point to the
  IP. Ask whoever hosts the IP to fix the PTR record, or fix the A record of that name.
- **A shared IP isn't on the page.** Shared pools are checked at domain level; the sending provider monitors their
  IPs.
