New Amazon SES accounts start in the SES sandbox in every AWS Region. This guide shows how to file an Amazon SES
production access request from ReputeCast's setup wizard, what to write in it, and how to raise your sending quota once
AWS has approved it.

## What the sandbox means

While a Region of your AWS account is in the sandbox, Amazon SES delivers only to verified addresses and domains (or
the SES mailbox simulator), at most 200 messages per 24 hours and 1 message per second. Sandbox status is separate for
each Region ([Amazon SES Developer Guide, "Request production access"](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/request-production-access.html),
checked October 2026).

ReputeCast detects it for you:

- When you connect Amazon SES, ReputeCast reads your account in every Region and shows which ones already have
  production access, so you can pick one of those under <UiPath path="connect.connection.ses.regionLabel" />.
- A connection whose Region is still in the sandbox shows the status
  <UiPath path="providers.statusSandbox" /> under <UiPath path="nav.providers" />.
- Send Readiness blocks a campaign whose route uses a sandboxed provider, in its
  <UiPath path="review.groupRouting" /> group, because the mail would reach only verified addresses.
- The provider health check reads the account again every 5 minutes, so the status changes by itself once AWS grants
  production access.

## Before you begin

- You need `providers:manage`, a sensitive permission. Owners and Admins have it.
- ReputeCast files the request with the SES call `PutAccountDetails`. The IAM policy that the CloudFormation stack
  creates, and the one shown for IAM access keys, already include `ses:PutAccountDetails`.
- Verify your sending domain first: AWS recommends it, and approved requests still need verified identities for every
  From and Return-Path address ([Add sending domains and senders](/providers/senders-and-domains/)).
- Have a public website for the publication and a description of how people sign up.

<Steps>

1. Open <UiPath path="nav.providers" /> and connect Amazon SES (see [Send with Amazon SES](/providers/amazon-ses/)).
   Request production access in the <UiPath path="connect.stepConnection" /> step.

2. Under <UiPath path="connect.connection.ses.regionLabel" />, pick the Region you will send from. When it is in the
   sandbox, a card names the Region and its sandbox quota and shows the request form.

3. Check the fields. ReputeCast fills them in from your workspace:

   - In <UiPath path="connect.connection.ses.website" />, the organization domain of your default sender.
   - In <UiPath path="connect.connection.ses.mailType" />, choose <UiPath path="connect.connection.ses.mailMarketing" />
     for newsletters, or <UiPath path="connect.connection.ses.mailTransactional" /> if most of your mail is one-to-one.
   - In <UiPath path="connect.connection.ses.useCase" />, your lists, the number of opted-in contacts, and how bounces,
     complaints and unsubscribes are handled. It takes 20 to 5,000 characters. Replace it with your own text (see
     [Request text you can copy](#request-text-you-can-copy)).
   - In <UiPath path="connect.connection.ses.dailyVolume" />, how many emails you expect to send a day. ReputeCast
     adds it to the description.
   - In <UiPath path="connect.connection.ses.contactLanguage" />, English or Japanese, the language AWS writes to you
     in.

4. Select the request button for that Region. ReputeCast files the request with your ReputeCast account's email
   address as the contact for AWS's answer, and records it in the audit log.

   send only to verified addresses; you can't edit the request while it is under review
   ([Amazon SES Developer Guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/request-production-access.html), checked
   October 2026).

</Steps>

## Request text you can copy

AWS reads the description to see that you send only to people who asked for your mail and that you handle bounces,
complaints and unsubscribes. Keep only the sentences that are true for your workspace, and fill in the parts in angle
brackets.

```text title="use-case.txt"
<Publication> is a newsletter about <topic> from <company>, sent from <sending domain> to people who
subscribed on <website>. New subscribers confirm their address by clicking a link in a confirmation
email (double opt-in) before they receive anything. We never buy, rent or share lists.

We send <one issue a week> to about <number> subscribers, around <number> emails a day.

We send through ReputeCast, a self-hosted newsletter platform. Amazon SES reports bounces and complaints
to it through Amazon SNS. Hard bounces and complaints put the address on a suppression list at once,
and it is never mailed again. A send pauses automatically when its bounce rate passes 2% or its
complaint rate passes 0.1%.

Every message has a visible unsubscribe link and List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post headers
for one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058). Unsubscribes take effect immediately.

Contacts who have not opened or clicked in <180> days stop receiving issues.
```

The facts behind these sentences: [Lists, consent and double opt-in](/audience/lists-and-consent/),
[Bounces, complaints and provider limits](/deliverability/bounces-and-complaints/),
[One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058)](/deliverability/one-click-unsubscribe/) and
[Keep your list healthy](/audience/list-health/).

## Request it in the AWS console instead

If the provider is already connected, or you prefer to file the request yourself, use the SES console. These are the
steps AWS documents ([Amazon SES Developer Guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/request-production-access.html),
checked October 2026):

<Steps>

1. Open the Amazon SES console in the Region you send from and choose **Account dashboard**.

2. In the box that says your account is in the sandbox, choose **View Get set up page**, then **Request production
   access**.

3. Choose **Marketing** or **Transactional**, enter your **Website URL**, up to 4 **Additional contacts** and the
   **Preferred contact language**.

4. Check the **Acknowledgement** box and choose **Submit request**.

</Steps>

The AWS CLI files the same request:

```sh title="Terminal"
aws sesv2 put-account-details \
  --production-access-enabled \
  --mail-type MARKETING \
  --website-url https://example.com \
  --additional-contact-email-addresses you@example.com \
  --contact-language EN
```

If AWS asks for more details, answer in the support case with the text above.

## Raise your sending quota

Production access comes with a daily sending quota and a maximum sending rate per Region. AWS raises them
automatically for accounts that send wanted mail close to their quota with low bounce and complaint rates. To ask for
more, open the Service Quotas console, choose **Amazon Simple Email Service (SES)** and request an increase of the
sending quota or the sending rate; AWS answers within 24 hours
([Amazon SES Developer Guide, "Increasing your Amazon SES sending quotas"](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/manage-sending-quotas-request-increase.html),
checked October 2026).

ReputeCast reads the new quota and rate with its next health check and shows them with the provider.

## Check that it worked

- The provider's status under <UiPath path="nav.providers" /> no longer shows
  <UiPath path="providers.statusSandbox" />.
- Send Readiness passes the bulk-marketing terms check in its <UiPath path="review.groupRouting" /> group.

## Troubleshooting

- **"AWS didn't take the request."** ReputeCast shows AWS's error, and nothing was filed. An access denied error means
  the IAM role or user lacks `ses:PutAccountDetails`; update the policy and try again.
- **The request was denied.** Read AWS's answer in the support case. Reply with how people subscribe and how you handle
  bounces and complaints, using the text above.
- **Production access in one Region, sandbox in another.** Sandbox status is per Region. Move the connection to the
  Region with production access, or file a request for the other one.
- **Approved, but the status still says sandbox.** Wait for the next health check, at most 5 minutes.

<Aside type="tip">
Production access doesn't warm up a new dedicated IP. If you send from dedicated IPs, start a
[warm-up plan](/providers/warm-up/) before your first large campaign.
</Aside>
