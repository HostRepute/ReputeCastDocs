Email deliverability is whether your mail reaches the inbox instead of the spam folder or a bounce. Mailbox providers
such as Gmail, Yahoo and Outlook.com decide that for every message, from the signals below. When you self-host, you
choose the sending providers and own the DNS, so these signals are yours to manage; ReputeCast checks them before,
while and after each send.

ReputeCast tells which [mailbox provider](/reference/glossary/#mailbox-provider) filters each address from its
domain's MX records, not only from its name: a company domain on Google Workspace counts as Gmail, one on Microsoft 365
as Outlook. Routing, warm-up, guarded sending, Send Readiness and reports all use that one answer.

## How ReputeCast acts before, while and after sending

<SendFlowDiagram />

Send Readiness checks a campaign before it starts, routing hands each recipient to a sending provider, delivery events
come back from the sending providers, and thresholds decide whether the send keeps going or pauses.

1. **Before you send.** Domain records are checked live while you publish them and every 30 minutes after that. [Send
   Readiness](/reference/glossary/#send-readiness) checks each campaign in up to 11 groups: sender reputation (the
   sending domain, its parent, return-path and tracking domains, and dedicated IPs), authentication, content, languages,
   accessibility, conditional content, links, compliance, audience quality, routing and your workspace's own [sending
   rules](/campaigns/sending-rules/). Its verdict is Ready, Ready with warnings or Blocked, and a Block keeps the send
   from starting. A check that couldn't run, such as a DNS lookup that failed, is
   <UiPath path="review.statusUnknown" />, never a <UiPath path="review.statusBlock" />. It runs when you open
   <UiPath path="composer.reviewSend" />, again 15 minutes before a scheduled send, and when the send starts. At that
   recheck a block, a warning nobody confirmed, a large audience change or an edit made after scheduling holds the send
   ([send-time hold](/reference/glossary/#send-time-hold)) until someone reviews it. A [placement
   test](/deliverability/placement-tests/) shows where real copies land in seed mailboxes, per mailbox provider.
2. **While you send.** A send can start with a [canary](/reference/glossary/#canary): the most engaged recipients and
   your seed mailboxes get it first, and everyone else waits until it passes. [Guarded
   sending](/reference/glossary/#guarded-sending) then watches bounces by type, complaints and deferrals per mailbox
   provider and per sending provider every 2 seconds, slows a mailbox provider down on deferrals before it pauses, and
   [auto-pauses](/campaigns/live-monitor/) only the part that crossed a threshold. Complaint rates count only mail
   delivered to mailbox providers that report complaints: Gmail and iCloud don't, so theirs read
   <UiPath path="live.model.notAvailable" />. The thresholds, the rates per mailbox provider and reply rules such as
   Yahoo's `TSS04` are set in [traffic rules](/providers/traffic-rules/). When a sending provider fails, the route's
   failover policy decides whether its share moves to a healthy one or waits for you.
3. **After you send.** Delivery events from every sending provider are normalized, and bounces and complaints go to one
   [suppression list](/reference/glossary/#suppression-list) that applies to every sending provider. A complaint watch
   keeps counting complaints after the send ends and alerts the people who can resume sends when a mailbox provider or
   the whole send passes its complaint limit. [Campaign reports](/campaigns/reports/) sort opens into human,
   [privacy-protected](/reference/glossary/#privacy-protected-open) and machine opens, and [Deliverability
   health](/deliverability/health/) keeps watching your domains, IPs, sending provider accounts, blocklists, DMARC
   reports and incidents.

## When to change them

The Send Readiness limits are fixed. What you decide is how to answer each result: fix a Block, and fix or confirm each
warning before you send. The thresholds that end a send early are yours to tune: auto-pause rates per workspace and per
campaign ([Monitor a send and auto-pause](/campaigns/live-monitor/)), and each sending provider's bounce and complaint
limits ([Provider health and outages](/providers/health-and-outages/)). Lower them when a mailbox provider starts
deferring or filtering your mail; raising them only lets more damage happen before the pause.

## Deliverability checklist

### Set up once

1. Publish SPF, DKIM and DMARC for every sending domain:
   [Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC](/deliverability/spf-dkim-dmarc/).
2. Collect DMARC reports and move to an enforced policy:
   [Read DMARC aggregate reports](/deliverability/dmarc-reports/).
3. Check the rules of the large mailbox providers:
   [Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft bulk sender requirements](/deliverability/bulk-sender-requirements/).
4. Make sure one-click unsubscribe works, which needs an HTTPS public URL:
   [One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058)](/deliverability/one-click-unsubscribe/).
5. Collect consent with evidence and keep a postal address in the footer:
   [Consent and anti-spam law basics](/deliverability/compliance/).
6. With Amazon SES, leave the sandbox before the first campaign:
   [Move Amazon SES out of the sandbox](/deliverability/amazon-ses-production-access/).
7. Warm up a new sending provider or IP before full volume: [Warm up a new provider or IP](/providers/warm-up/).
8. Optional: show your logo with [BIMI](/deliverability/bimi/), and
   [connect HostRepute](/deliverability/hostrepute/) for hourly blocklist checks, Postmaster Tools, SNDS and DMARC
   collection.

### Before every send

9. Fix every Block and read every warning in [Send Readiness](/campaigns/send-readiness/).
10. For important sends, run a [placement test](/deliverability/placement-tests/) first.

### Keep watching

11. Open [Deliverability health](/deliverability/health/) regularly and act on amber and red cards.
12. Get listings removed quickly: [Check and fix blocklist listings](/deliverability/blocklists/).
13. Stay under the limits of each sending provider:
    [Bounces, complaints and provider limits](/deliverability/bounces-and-complaints/).
14. Stop mailing contacts who never engage: [Keep your list healthy](/audience/list-health/) and
    [The suppression list](/audience/suppressions/).

### When mail bounces

15. Look up the code a mailbox provider returned: [SMTP error codes](/smtp-errors/).
16. Find which mailbox providers and sending providers it affects in [campaign reports](/campaigns/reports/).
