Anti-spam and privacy laws set rules for every newsletter: who you may email, what each email must contain, and what
you owe people who ask about their data. This page summarizes the CAN-SPAM and GDPR newsletter requirements most
senders meet and shows where ReputeCast records, checks or enforces each one.

<Aside type="note" title="This is not legal advice">
This page describes common rules and the tools ReputeCast gives you. Which laws apply depends on where you and your
readers are. Ask a lawyer about your own obligations.
</Aside>

## What the laws ask

- **CAN-SPAM (United States)** applies to commercial email. Among other rules, it bans misleading headers and subject
  lines, and requires that each message identifies itself as an ad, includes your valid physical postal address, tells
  the recipient how to opt out, and that you honor an opt-out within 10 business days
  ([FTC, "CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business"](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business),
  checked October 2026).
- **GDPR (European Union)** applies when you process personal data of people in the EU. When you email them on the
  basis of consent, you must be able to show that each person consented (Article 7), give people a copy of their data
  when they ask (Article 15), erase it on request when the conditions apply (Article 17), and stop direct marketing
  when they object (Article 21)
  ([Regulation (EU) 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), checked October 2026).
- **Mailbox providers** add their own rules for bulk senders, such as authentication and one-click unsubscribe. See
  [Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft bulk sender requirements](/deliverability/bulk-sender-requirements/).

## How ReputeCast covers each one

1. **Consent with evidence.** Consent is stored per list: how the contact joined (form, import or a team member), when,
   from which IP address when they confirmed, and the version of the consent text they saw. Forms use
   [double opt-in](/reference/glossary/#double-opt-in) by default, and an import can mark contacts as pending until
   they confirm. Every change lands in the contact's consent history. See
   [Lists, consent and double opt-in](/audience/lists-and-consent/).
2. **A postal address and an unsubscribe link in every email.** The <UiPath path="review.groupCompliance" /> group of
   [Send Readiness](/reference/glossary/#send-readiness) is a <UiPath path="review.statusBlock" /> when the body of a
   campaign has no visible unsubscribe link or no postal address. Its one-click fix inserts the footer block, which
   holds the workspace's postal address and links to unsubscribe, manage preferences and view the email in a browser.
   Each language version needs its own footer.
3. **Opt-out that works at once.** Every Broadcast and Automation message carries the `List-Unsubscribe` and
   `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers, so mail apps can offer one-click unsubscribe, and the footer links to the
   unsubscribe and preference pages. An unsubscribe puts the address on the suppression list immediately, which is
   well inside CAN-SPAM's 10 business days. See [One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058)](/deliverability/one-click-unsubscribe/)
   and [Subscribe, preference and unsubscribe pages](/forms/public-pages/).
4. **Honest headers.** Campaigns send from verified sender addresses on domains you authenticate with SPF, DKIM and
   DMARC ([Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC](/deliverability/spf-dkim-dmarc/)). The compliance group warns when the sending
   domain doesn't pass DMARC yet and, with Google Postmaster Tools data from [HostRepute](/deliverability/hostrepute/),
   shows Gmail's own compliance status for the domain
   ([bulk sender requirements](/deliverability/bulk-sender-requirements/)).
5. **Access requests.** <UiPath path="audience.profile.exportData" /> on a contact's profile downloads everything
   ReputeCast stores about that person as JSON: the profile and custom fields, consent evidence, every message with its
   stream and delivery status, the activity timeline and any suppression. It needs `subscribers:export`, and each
   export is recorded in the audit log.
6. **Erasure.** Select contacts under <UiPath path="nav.audience > audience.tabContacts" />, then
   <UiPath path="audience.contacts.delete" />, and type the number of contacts to confirm. Names, fields, tags, list
   memberships, consent history and preferences are removed, and the address is replaced; campaign statistics keep
   their counts without the person. It needs `subscribers:delete`.

<Aside type="caution" title="Erasing can't be undone">
Erasing removes the consent history for good, and it doesn't add the address to the suppression list. When a person
asks you to forget them and never email them again, select <UiPath path="audience.profile.suppress" /> on their
profile first, then erase them, so a later import can't bring the address back.
</Aside>

## When to change them

- Keep double opt-in on. Turn it off for a form only if you are sure the law that applies to your readers allows
  single opt-in, and you still record where each signup came from.
- Export a contact's data before you erase it when the person asked for both.
- Review consent text whenever your publication or what you send changes, because each signup records the version it
  was given.
